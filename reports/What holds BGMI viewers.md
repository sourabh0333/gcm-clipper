# Payoffs, people and talk hold BGMI viewers

Viewers of Goblin, Joker and LoLzZz do not watch a highlight from start to end. They watch the first 10-15 seconds, then skim to short spikes about once a minute. **Each spike is a payoff with a reaction attached**: a kill burst with a shout, a clutch, the final fight into the Chicken Dinner, or a funny line with a laugh or a reply. Driving, looting, holding in smoke and chat with no punchline get skipped, even in the creators' own edits. When viewers name a moment in the comments, **half the time it is talk, not a kill**. The kills they do name win the biggest likes, and they praise *how* he pushed, never the count. Openings on a voice line or gunfire hold; slow openings lose. Endings hold when the video stops seconds after the result or the last laugh. In Shorts since 2023, the subject decides reach: every sampled Short above 2M views is a person moment. For Kaatclip this means four main changes: keep the Dinner out of the cold open, score talk as highly as kills, end on the reaction sentence rather than a fixed time, and choose Shorts by people first. Most of Kaatclip's 14 Nov behaviour already matches the six channels. This report builds on [BGMI creators content A to Z](BGMI%20creators%20content%20A%20to%20Z.md) and does not repeat it.

**Sources.** Five studies in `research_notes/BGMI what holds viewers/`: [heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md) (779 videos), [comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md) (9,593), [Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md) (171), [openings and endings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md) (93), [frame timing](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md) (15), plus 3 videos [watched earlier](../research_notes/BGMI%20creators%20content%20A%20to%20Z/editing_timing_watched.md). All public YouTube data; no audio was heard. The "most replayed" heatmap is scaled 0-1 inside each video, and a peak can mean viewers skipped to it as well as replayed it. It shows where attention gathers, not how many stayed.

## 1. Summary for the owner: the 10 things that hold viewers

1. **Everyone watches the start, and half are gone within about 14 seconds.** In 779 long videos heat halves after a median of 13.7 s and settles to a low baseline by 8% of the length ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). The first seconds must already be a payoff.
2. **After that, viewers skim between spikes.** A median video has 4 peaks, about 15 s wide, 72 s apart; half the body sits near zero ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). A highlight is a chain of 15-25 s payoff windows, and the bridges are skip-bait.
3. **A peak is an event plus a reaction.** Kill bursts (18 of 59 main peaks), funny lines with a laugh (15), the final fight and result (13), reveals (7), clutches (6), each with a shout, laugh or face ([PtvEiSN48Dk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PtvEiSN48Dk): Jonathan laughing after the decisive 1v1).
4. **Viewers name talk more than kills.** Of 393 moments that viewers quoted by timestamp, 51% are talk or people and 17% are skill. Results and Dinners are just 2%. In 31 of 57 videos the most-liked timestamp is a line or banter ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)).
5. **But skill moments win the biggest likes, and the praise is for the push.** Skill is 17% of moments but 35% of timestamp likes, and 72% on Goblin's main channel. The top moment overall (869 likes) is "Bhai itna confidence lata kaha se h" on a solo push into a squad ([67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI) at 9:58). Keep the run-in, not just the kill.
6. **The line said after a fight is what people repeat.** "Legit: Or Kidhar Hai?? Joker: Lobby Mein" (445 likes over the moment, [xiqm0RdJR3o](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xiqm0RdJR3o) at 8:20 of 8:29). Cutting right after the last kill throws away the most quoted part. Viewers mock it when it happens: "ek baar kya 1v3 maar diyaa isne to reel hi kaat dii" (188 likes, [TVZ2RLVXD7E](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/TVZ2RLVXD7E)).
7. **Open on a voice or gunfire, never slowly.** Heat just after the start (p2, relative to the video's mean) is 1.73 for a funny line or face, 1.50 for a fight already running, about 1.25 for teasers and goal lines, and 0.41 for slow starts like lobby greetings or a plane ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)).
8. **Kill and Dinner videos never open on the result.** None of 93 opens on its WWCD. Videos that hold the promised result back end at 1.84x their mean heat, against 0.92x for those that show it. The suspense is "how", not "whether" ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)).
9. **End within seconds of the payoff.** Ending on the climax keeps the last point at 58% of the climax; a tail up to 20 s keeps 22%; longer tails 5-6%. End cards and abrupt cuts lose almost everyone ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)).
10. **In Shorts, the subject beats the edit.** All 11 sampled Shorts above 2M views since 2023 are person moments: a friend shocked, a prank, a rant, a 1v1 against a named pro. The best pure-skill Shorts stop at 1.5-1.8M, and generic kill clips sit at 10-20K ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)). Frame 1 must already be the action or the line, and there must be no dead tail.

One finding sits over all of these. **The heatmap's shape does not predict views.** Across 779 videos, opening strength, end strength and peak count all correlate with views at |ρ| ≤ 0.05 ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). Editing decides whether viewers who arrive stay and come back. Title, thumbnail and the people in the video decide whether they arrive at all. Kaatclip needs both levers, and they are separate.

## 2. What viewers watch and want

### Viewers jump to payoffs, and the reaction is part of the payoff

The replay curves show the same shape on all six channels. Heat around a peak runs 0.11 at 30 s before it, 0.44 at 10 s before, 1.0 at the peak, 0.31 at 10 s after and 0.16 at 20 s after. **A peak decays the same way whether it sits mid-video or at the end** ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). The moment viewers come for lasts about 15 s, and they leave it within 10-20 s.

What sits at the peaks depends on the creator. On Goblin's main channel, **6 of 12 main peaks are the final fight and the WWCD** ([67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI), 1.0 at 96%). Joker's are group laughs, roasts and prank payoffs ([TIDXJ1YeirM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIDXJ1YeirM), [Du8VpIvMs04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Du8VpIvMs04)). LoLzZz's are mid-match kill bursts with a facecam cut or meme, plus the record payoff at the end ([kOccoMYydwI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOccoMYydwI)). Almost every peak pairs the event with a reaction: head in hands after the final kills ([fbVXWfefL8c](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fbVXWfefL8c)), a laugh after a squad wipe ([Ul5mEjNyzu4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ul5mEjNyzu4)), screams over a clutch ([1bSJjsh33kM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1bSJjsh33kM)).

The flat parts are just as consistent. The longest runs below 0.08 in 12 edited videos lasted **47 to 203 s**: car rotations ([7bmUiiUuAdE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bmUiiUuAdE)), holding a compound ([67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI), 400-562 s), creeping in smoke ([6jIQaYvIOGc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6jIQaYvIOGc), 113-160 s), looting, and two-facecam chat with no punchline ([3c0nLXL8k6U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3c0nLXL8k6U)). **A meme on a flat stretch did not lift it** ([z1jXPJN8z-c](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z1jXPJN8z-c) at 4:39). The human editors keep these stretches, so Kaatclip can cut harder than they do.

### People over kills: what the comments say viewers want

The comments study coded 393 timestamped moments from 9,593 comments ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)). The split shows what viewers care about enough to point at:

| Moment type | Share of moments | Share of likes |
|---|---|---|
| Funny line | 25% | 17% |
| Banter between players | 16% | 24% |
| Kill or kill burst | 14% | 29% |
| Funny gameplay (fail, chase) | 7% | 5% |
| A named person showing up | 6% | 4% |
| Editor meme insert | 4% | 4% |
| Movement, clutch, game-sense call | 3% | 6% |
| Result, Dinner, record | 2% | 3% |

Two things stand out. **Talk is half of what viewers name**, and two thirds or more on Joker's channels (JokerClips: 67% of moments, 84% of likes). Goblin's main channel is the exception: skill is 45% of moments and 72% of likes there. And **the result screen is almost never quoted**, even where it is a heatmap peak. Viewers watch the Dinner but talk about the push and the line. Timestamps and replays agree: timestamped moments sit at the **74th percentile** of their own video's replay curve (skill 84th, talk 74th) ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)).

People run through everything. **Viewers rename videos after a teammate's catchphrase**: "The title should be 6 minutes of Akshat saying BADHIYAAA" (298 likes, [qy_BVesBY_M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qy_BVesBY_M)). Rivalry comments carry the largest likes: "No Competition is liye Soul vs Soul ho rha hai" (4,300, [DfOcogRMngg](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DfOcogRMngg)). Videos under 150K views get twice as much plain cheering and half as many quoted lines as videos over 300K. **A video that is only "we won" gives fans nothing to repeat**, like a 39K WWCD cut whose 18 comments mention nothing in it ([xtE3WWqt1_E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xtE3WWqt1_E)).

### Talk after fights is the most quoted footage

**The last third of long videos holds 47% of timestamp likes**; the final 60 s alone holds 21%, mostly funny lines and banter ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)). Many are replies said just after a fight: Joker's "lobby mein" as the round is won, or "pagla gaya vo" at [7TkLt-j3u4A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7TkLt-j3u4A) 12:54, that video's best-liked moment, with its heatmap peak at 12:58.

Bloopers count too: "ye nahi dalna tha" (87 likes) marks the top replay peak of [9tOJ5bdmkWE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9tOJ5bdmkWE). The only real complaints are about **moments the live audience remembers being cut** ("Bathroom wala scene missing hai", [fCCQkW1Uevk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fCCQkW1Uevk)); 77 comments say they watched it live. Nobody complained about cuts, length or clickbait, and "full match" requests are 13 in 9,589. The one sound complaint is **music under gameplay**; the game's "ting ting" headshot sound is loved (516 likes, [C76kBoyodHY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C76kBoyodHY)).

### The promised moment must be there, whole

**Viewers jump to what the title promises.** In "Chicken dinner with pan" ([0Ug9nHMiD24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Ug9nHMiD24)), heat is 0.0-0.1 from 5% to 85% and 1.0 at the pan kill and WWCD at 95% ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). Kaatclip's titles promise the Dinner and the kill count, so the Dinner must be there whole, and the title number must equal the final screen.

## 3. Openings: the first 1, 3, 10 and 30 seconds

### The only opening that clearly loses is a slow one

The openings study labelled the first 60 s of 93 videos and compared their heatmaps ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)):

| Opening | n | p2 | f10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funny line or face at 0 s | 9 | **1.73** | **1.73** |
| Fight already running | 22 | 1.50 | 1.38 |
| Teaser montage | 32 | 1.25 | 1.36 |
| Goal or stakes line | 14 | 1.26 | 1.15 |
| Slow cold start (lobby, plane, context) | 4 | **0.41** | **0.88** |

(p2 is the heat at 1-2% into the video and f10 the mean of the first 10%, both divided by the video's mean.) Only 7 of 84 videos have their first point below their own mean, and **every one of the 7 opens slowly**: talk with no hook, a lobby, a broadcast map, a black-and-white face teaser or a logo ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)). The heatmaps study found the differences between the fast styles are within noise ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). Together: **any opening on a voice line or gunfire is fine; a slow one loses.** The strongest openings are a fight with comms at 0 s (p2 4.76 on [6jIQaYvIOGc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6jIQaYvIOGc), 4.87 on [khbJdsEChm0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=khbJdsEChm0)) and the rules of a cash challenge (4.05, [7bmUiiUuAdE](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7bmUiiUuAdE)). The weakest include 35 s of a broadcast's plane (0.42, [nkvIKh72uH8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkvIKh72uH8)) and 20 s of lobby greetings (0.41, [Xzzw4S0p3l0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xzzw4S0p3l0)).

**The first words should be the line the title and thumbnail promise.** Openings that show the promised line or moment at once have f10 1.42, against 1.23 for a hint (on Joker's channels 1.68-1.79 against 0.97-1.17). The best hold in the sample, [3QRE7nzZvaM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3QRE7nzZvaM) (f10 2.23), opens on exactly the thumbnail's speech bubble.

### Teasers: short, from the body, never the Dinner

Teasers appear in 35 of 93 videos. **The median is 22 s** (Joker 12-20, Goblin 8-35, LoLzZz 22-50). Their lines come from 13-75% of the video; only 8 of 26 traced teasers use a line from the last 10%, and 6 of those are LoLzZz's. His record teasers say the final number ("72" at 43 s on [-qpx-ejuq9Q](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-qpx-ejuq9Q)) but never show the record kill ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)).

**Long cold opens get skipped.** In 18 of 38 videos whose story starts 10 s or more in, the heatmap spikes at the second the story begins: viewers seeking past the intro. It is strongest for stories starting at 21-35 s (9 of 16), and weakest on Joker's 12-18 s teasers. The extreme is a 45 s face talk followed by a 14x spike at the duel ([cQRWK4Z0Yu8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cQRWK4Z0Yu8)). So **a teaser should run 12-20 s, never over 30 s.** The measured teasers switch by hard cuts: LoLzZz's duo teasers hold faces 2.5-4 s while talking and POVs 1-2 s on action ([5GNFp6GqCso](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5GNFp6GqCso)), with one blurred frame at most going into gameplay. Goblin's hero edits close the teaser with one fade to black before the match ([67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI) 9.25-11.5 s) ([frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md)).

### Where the studies disagree: should the cold open show the Dinner?

Kaatclip's cold open now ends on the Dinner banner, at the owner's request ("the intro is the most crucial part"). The openings study says not to, and its evidence is strong. **No video in 93 opens on its WWCD, VICTORY or record footage.** [67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI) teases kills, keeps its WWCD for 15 s before the end, and has end5 5.2x with its heatmap maximum at 96%. Videos that withhold the promised result end at 1.84x their mean, against 0.92x for those that show it ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)).

The resolution: **keep the promise, hold the footage**. The hook "15 KILLS + CHICKEN DINNER 🏆" stays, since a written or spoken result is fine (LoLzZz says his number; the thumbnail says it anyway). The banner and the final kill wait for the end, so viewers stay to see *how*. The fourth beat becomes the best mid-match fight or the funniest line with its reply, and the cold open ends on a laugh or a knock call. The intro stays fast and loaded; it just stops spending the ending.

### Openings by video type

| Type | First 1-3 s | By 10 s | By 30 s | Do not |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dinner or kill match | A fight already running with the call ("chal chal") | First knock, or a 12-20 s teaser of fights and one line | The match, from the first real fight | Show the WWCD or final kill; open on the plane or looting |
| Funny session | The funniest line, said in full with a subtitle | An "IN THIS VIDEO" teaser of 3-4 lines, 12-18 s, ending on a laugh | The first bit; greetings and earphone checks cut | Open on "hello guys" or setup talk |
| Custom room or vs streamer | The stake line or the opponent noticing ("hamare match mein LoLs aa gaye") | Rules in one sentence, then gunfire | The first round or duel | 45 s of talk before play |
| Crate | The stake ("60000 UC daal di") | The first pull | Pulls with reactions | Show the jackpot first (a 1-2 s flash at most) |
| Kill record | The goal in his own voice ("aaj target 70") | 2-3 teaser kills, 3-5 s each | The number, then the drop by about 30 s | Run a 30-60 s cold open |

Sources: [openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md) (per-type table and examples [G4dIkpIpLOw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G4dIkpIpLOw), [3i83F-jSotA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3i83F-jSotA), [l2o5yycZ9HY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l2o5yycZ9HY), [bCJbLnqdfOQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bCJbLnqdfOQ)); [heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md) (crate rule 11).

Two warnings. **Black first frames are a LoLzZz habit, not a rule**: black-first openings do slightly worse (p2 1.25 against 1.37). **The drop is not a hook**: plane openings score 0.42, and the skip spike shows that viewers past a teaser want action, not a parachute ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)). Kaatclip's 10 s look at the drop (used when a match has no early fight) should carry a spoken plan or stake, or go.

## 4. The body: rhythm, cuts, timing and effects

### Rhythm: a payoff about every minute

The median gap between peaks is **72 s** (52-62 s on Goblin and JokerClips, 92-109 s on LoLzZz). In 10-20 minute videos the best fight drifts to the middle: the highest point falls at 40-70% in 37% of them ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). A peak runs from 10-15 s before the event to 5-10 s after. So the unit of a highlight is **a payoff window of 15-25 s, joined to the next with as little bridge as possible.**

The skipped footage is the same on every channel: driving, rotation, holding in smoke or compounds, looting, scoping with no kill, crate spins with no jackpot, and chat with no punchline. Lobby chat earns a place only with a laugh or banter between named people ([U21BOYlKr2g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U21BOYlKr2g), 0.92). Kaatclip already compresses this (pieces under 18 s apart merged, longer gaps cut, "A FEW MINUTES LATER" over 150 s).

### How fights are cut: one take, from the call to the reaction

**Across all 18 measured videos, no fight is cut inside**: no zooms on kills, no slow motion, replays, freezes, shakes or flash frames ([frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md)). Goblin's final fight in [67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI) (589-612 s) and the 8.29M Short [Css0AXuRBZQ](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Css0AXuRBZQ) each run as one take. The bursts seen at kills come from the game's gun skins. Goblin's stream cut [wDgeZBn80VM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wDgeZBn80VM) turned a match into 4 stretches of 87, 26, 71 and 162 s, each starting on the call or the first shots ([watched](../research_notes/BGMI%20creators%20content%20A%20to%20Z/editing_timing_watched.md)).

A fight must start early enough to include the push ("confidence", "movement") and end late enough to include the first sentence after the last kill, because that line is what viewers quote ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)). A measurable rule: **end no earlier than the end of the first sentence after the last kill plus 1.5 s, capped at 20 s past the last shot**, and never cut away sooner than 0.5 s after a kill frame ([5GNFp6GqCso](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5GNFp6GqCso) 417.5 → 418.0 s). Kaatclip's fight piece (back 12-16 s for the call, 6 s tail, up to 10 s of reaction) is close already.

**A disagreement to settle: "never cut inside a fight" against cutting dead stretches.** Kaatclip cuts stretches of 20 s or more inside a fight with no loud gunfire, no kill and no talk. The frame study sampled fights around peaks; the heatmap shows that viewers skip exactly these holds (creeping in smoke for 47 s, holding a compound for 162 s). A silent 20 s hold is not fighting to the viewer. Keep the rule, with the cut on quiet sound as now. Where the cut jumps the player's position, a 1.5 s face shot with talk can hide it, as LoLzZz's editor does ([PRaUeGY8Sr8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PRaUeGY8Sr8) 41.0-42.5 s).

**Talk is cut at the end of a line, or 0.2-0.5 s before the next one**, never mid-sentence ([tt1gwGtxjlU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tt1gwGtxjlU) at 6.25 and 15.5 s). Kaatclip's cuts (quietest moment within 1.2 s, never inside a word, 80/150 ms fades) match this.

### Faces: three different moves, all tied to words

The **punch-in on a key word** is a tighter crop for about 1.25 s, from 0.1 s before the word to 0.1 s after, with hard cuts ("A Gulrez", [tt1gwGtxjlU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tt1gwGtxjlU) 3.0-4.25 s; only one example timed).

The **full-screen face for a line** is a hard cut to the face, held for the line, then back ([Kobcv7gzuAY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kobcv7gzuAY) 23.25-25.5 s; in Shorts 3-15 s, [LCZiW8i8zjk](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LCZiW8i8zjk)). Kaatclip's face punch-in (full screen on a sentence with no gunfire, held for the sentence, with subtitles) is this move, and it matches.

The **reaction cutaway** is 1.25-2.5 s of face on a shout or laugh right after the action: Jonathan shouting at [5GNFp6GqCso](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5GNFp6GqCso) 129.75-131.0 s (heat 1.0), LoLzZz's hand on his head after VICTORY at [kOccoMYydwI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOccoMYydwI) 526.5-528.5 s. A variant is the **black-and-white face for despair**, 2.5-8 s over the line; the 94-kill record video's heatmap maximum is one ([PRaUeGY8Sr8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PRaUeGY8Sr8) 258.5-266.5 s, "mere se nahi ho raha... kal karte") ([frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md)). Kaatclip has neither. Its punch-ins need a sentence with no gunfire, which skips the shout right after the last kill: a peak moment.

### Effects: allowed on words and reactions, never on kills

| Effect | Seen where | Allowed when |
|---|---|---|
| Zoom, slow-mo, shake, boom or flash on a kill | Nowhere in 18 videos | Never |
| Hard cut | Everywhere | Default between pieces |
| Fade to black (0.75 s down, about 1.25 s held) | Goblin, once per video | Once, after the cold open |
| Whip or zoom-blur (≤0.25-0.5 s) | LoLzZz only | Talk or lobby into gameplay; never gameplay to gameplay |
| Black beat (0.5-0.75 s) | [DfOcogRMngg](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DfOcogRMngg) | Joining two clips that tell a story |
| Face cutaway, punch-in, black-and-white face | LoLzZz, JokerClips, Goblin Shorts | On a word, a shout, a laugh, a despair line |
| Meme insert, sticker, freeze | LoLzZz, Joker, Goblin Shorts | On top of a real payoff or punchline only |
| Music | Montage videos | Never under fights or speech |

Sources: [frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md); [comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md) (memes 67% in the top replay quartile, "Meme ki timing ek dum badiya the"; the music complaints); [heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md) (memes over kills peak, memes on flat stretches do not).

Two of Kaatclip's effects fall outside this table. **The whoosh between cold-open beats** has no visual match: measured teasers switch by hard cuts, and LoLzZz's one whip sits at the hand-off into gameplay. Sound was not heard, so this is low confidence: one whoosh at the hand-off fits, three between beats do not. **The slow push-in on the Dinner banner** appears in no measured video; Goblin's WWCD in [67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI) (613-615 s) is plain footage. In a 16:9 stream layout a 1.12x centre push-in also crops the frame edges, where the facecam usually sits, at the second of the reaction.

**Subtitles in Roman Hinglish.** Only 0.4% of comments use Devanagari ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)), and Kaatclip's Hinglish model already writes Latin script. Subtitles are a house style: none of the 15 newly measured videos had them in the body, Joker uses them in teasers, LoLzZz on key lines in Shorts. Kaatclip's choice (talk pieces and funny lines only) fits.

## 5. Endings by video type

### Three studies, three answers, one rule

The comments study says end on the last laugh: results are 1.8% of quoted moments, while the final 60 s holds 21% of timestamp likes ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)). The heatmap study says end 3-8 s after the result card: the last point keeps 58% of the climax when the video ends on it, 5-6% with tails over 20 s ([heatmaps](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/heatmaps_long.md)). The frame study says editors hold the result 5-24 s: 11 s after the WWCD in [67w15TWdikI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67w15TWdikI), and a 24 s scoreboard plus 12 s of celebration in [PRaUeGY8Sr8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PRaUeGY8Sr8) ([frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md)).

They fit once you look at the screen. In a Dinner match the reaction line comes as the banner appears ("lobby mein" was said as the round was won), so "the last laugh" and "3-8 s after the card" are the same few seconds. The frame study shows what editors do; the heatmap shows viewers leave the extra seconds within 10-20 s. Records differ, because viewers stay for the number ([PRaUeGY8Sr8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PRaUeGY8Sr8) holds about 0.5 as the count reaches 94). So: **stop at the end of the reaction to the payoff, and let the payoff decide how long that is.**

## 6. Shorts

### Subject first: person moments win reach

**Since 2023, every sampled Short above 2M views is a person or relationship moment** ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)): Snax shocked by an outfit (8.63M, [GmZty_6yrQs](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/GmZty_6yrQs)), Goldy angry about food (5.48M, [9gy3caHoOMs](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/9gy3caHoOMs)), a revenge story (4.52M, [DfOcogRMngg](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DfOcogRMngg)), Goblin vs Neyo (3.42M, [5QoaTqu_u58](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/5QoaTqu_u58)), Jonathan asleep at a LAN (3.26M, [zC2CyyUHKG0](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zC2CyyUHKG0)). The best pure-skill Shorts since 2023 reach 1.76M and 1.48M ([OcYQ_xrdiaM](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OcYQ_xrdiaM)); clean clutches hit 5-9M only in the 2020-21 boom. Generic kill clips ("Frags 👿🔥", "EASY 1v2") sit at 10-13K. Fails count: ending on his own "ELIMINATED #15" drew 921K ([uCmDja_0DIE](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/uCmDja_0DIE)). Comments agree: Shorts comments quote the line or reaction ("I can hear his laugh !!", 1,200 likes), while kill-only tournament Shorts drew cheering, and a WWCD Short got 16K ([Wl187oS_j9I](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Wl187oS_j9I)) ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)).

**A disagreement to settle: kills in long videos, people in Shorts.** On Goblin's main channel skill is 72% of timestamp likes, so in a highlight the kill is the product. In Shorts the feed decides reach, and since 2023 it rewards people. Both hold: rank highlight moments with a strong skill weight, and Shorts by people and reactions first. A Dinner or kill Short qualifies only with a reaction, rivalry or clutch, as [OcYQ_xrdiaM](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OcYQ_xrdiaM) has (1.48M, ending on the WWCD).

### The first second, the payoff and the ending

**Frame 1 is already the action or the line.** Speech starts at a median of 0.2 s; 3% of tops start on a dark frame against 15% of bottoms; no top opens on text on black, a lobby or a parachute ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)). Examples: muzzle flash in frame 1 of [-vbAZpx5Iek](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-vbAZpx5Iek), "Joker bhai suno" at 0.0 s in [CVqRiZ-wwNw](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CVqRiZ-wwNw) ([frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md)). An instant visual payoff (someone caught asleep) goes in the first second; otherwise open on the setup line.

**The payoff lands in the middle or last third.** In the 17 Shorts with a second heatmap peak, it sits at 18-89% of the length, mostly 30-80%, on the payoff ([TVZ2RLVXD7E](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/TVZ2RLVXD7E): kill at 16 s, peak at 18 s of 22).

**The ending is a reaction or a proof screen, then the cut.** After the last kill, editors keep 1.0-1.5 s of game, then a 1.25-3.5 s tail: the face alone over a blur ([DfOcogRMngg](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DfOcogRMngg) 32.5-36 s), a meme clip ([CVqRiZ-wwNw](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/CVqRiZ-wwNw)) or colour bars ([UhUauBjjrn8](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/UhUauBjjrn8)) ([frames](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/editing_frames.md)). Proof screens also end tops ("#1 WINNER WINNER CHICKEN DINNER", [OcYQ_xrdiaM](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/OcYQ_xrdiaM)). **Dead tails go with flops**: 28% of bottoms end on 3 s or more of black or card, against 6% of tops; in one LoLzZz era, 8 of 10 bottoms end on 10-11 s "LIKE & SUBSCRIBE" cards while 10 of 12 tops end within 1 s of gameplay ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)). Never stop on the kill frame ("reel hi kaat dii").

**Length follows the moment**: tops median 39 s, bottoms 36.5 s, hits 15-60 s. Default 20-45 s, up to 60 s for a clutch or story.

### Layout and text

Game and face stacked is standard (game on top for Goblin and LoLzZz, face on top for Joker). **The best Shorts switch to the face alone for talk and reactions**, on a sentence, never mid-shooting ([LCZiW8i8zjk](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/LCZiW8i8zjk), [HhcQY2wm8Ug](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/HhcQY2wm8Ug)); Kaatclip already does. A dark, hooded face is worse than none: such Shorts drew 10-17K, and 6 of Goblin's 15 tops have no cam ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)). Text is house style: Goblin uses none and reaches 0.8-4.5M; Joker one fixed 2-4 word chip with an emoji ("1v4 Clutch 😡"); LoLzZz yellow key-line subtitles. Kaatclip's fixed hook band fits, but its words should name the person or situation.

### How many, from where, and when

**Shorts come from three places** ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)): the long video's opening beat (6 of 10 matched pairs, posted 1-2 h after it), a moment inside the highlight, and a moment straight from the stream. **That last group holds the biggest hits**: Goldy's tantrum (5.48M, from a Chained Together stream) and an IRL moment (836K) never became long videos. Creators post 2-4 per good stream. **Promo Shorts flop**: "Click Here to watch full video" sits at 7-13K. And if the highlight withholds the Dinner, a Dinner Short posted first spoils it; LoLzZz's record proof Short went up 5 h *after* the main video ([FyHDoGMnHO4](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/FyHDoGMnHO4)). Do not tune by likes per view: bottoms get 0.118, tops 0.064, because unpushed Shorts reach mostly fans.

## 7. Titles and thumbnails

**Thumbnails say what happened, the opening proves it is about to happen, and the ending delivers it** ([openings](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/openings_endings.md)). Of 93 thumbnails, 83 show a face, 70 carry big text and 41 a number. The number is always the outcome, and it always equals the number on the final screen. Every LoLzZz record thumbnail reads "N SOLO KILLS WORLD RECORD!!" over a "shh" pose. Duels show both faces with the score ("39-39" on [C76kBoyodHY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C76kBoyodHY)). Comedy shows faces with a speech bubble quoting the line; all 12 speech bubbles in the sample are on Joker's channels. Goblin's 2026 main uploads use raw game frames and still draw 134-272K, which works for an established name. Titles repeat the thumbnail: "Solo 14 Finishes | 29 Team Finishes domination" with "Solo 14 Finishes" on the image.

People lift titles. The earlier channel study found that named teammates, rivals or co-stars raise median views by about 1.2x to 2x ([A to Z](BGMI%20creators%20content%20A%20to%20Z.md)). The new studies add to that. Viewers retitle videos after a catchphrase ([qy_BVesBY_M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qy_BVesBY_M)). A named person or team appears in the titles of 11 of the 35 most-viewed sampled Shorts. Result-only titles on low-stakes matches drew cheering and little else ([xtE3WWqt1_E](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xtE3WWqt1_E), 39K; [Wl187oS_j9I](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Wl187oS_j9I), 16K) ([comments](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/comments.md)). Good Shorts titles name the person or situation in 2-6 words plus one emoji: "Goldy Dada Gussa Ho Gaye 🤣", "JONATHAN CAUGHT SLEEPING AT LAN 😭💀" ([Shorts](../research_notes/BGMI%20what%20holds%20viewers/shorts.md)).

Kaatclip's title format ("CHICKEN DINNER WITH 15 FINISHES | ERANGEL | BGMI") is the Goblin pattern and is safe for a Dinner. Its weak spot is that people's names are found only rarely, and people are the strongest lever in the whole study. Its thumbnails (three kill frames with the facecam cut out and stat words) match the face-plus-number pattern. Two things are missing. The face should come from the reaction moment (a shout, a laugh, hands on head), not only from the kill frame. And talk-led highlights have no line-based variant, such as Joker's speech bubble whose words are also the video's first line.

## 8. Kaatclip editing specification

Each rule is **KEEP** (Kaatclip already does it), **CHANGE** (does it differently) or **ADD** (missing), with one line of evidence. Numbers are starting values to tune on real streams.

### A. Ranking of moments

1. **KEEP.** The moment the title promises (Dinner, kill count) is in, whole. *Heat ~0 until the pan WWCD at 95% ([0Ug9nHMiD24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0Ug9nHMiD24)).*
2. **CHANGE.** Score talk on the same scale as kills, not only Gemini "funny 8+": a teammate's reply within 3 s, a named person, a repeated catchphrase, laughter. Weight by style: skill-heavy (Goblin) or talk-heavy (Joker, at least half the cut). *51% of quoted moments are talk; Goblin main skill 72% of likes, JokerClips talk 84%.*
3. **ADD.** A shout, laugh or line within 5 s raises a moment's score; a kill with no reaction ranks below one with it. *Peaks pair event and reaction (PtvEiSN48Dk, fbVXWfefL8c).*
4. **ADD.** Keep fails, deaths and bloopers that carry a reaction, with 5-10 s of aftermath. *"ye nahi dalna tha" is 9tOJ5bdmkWE's top peak; fail Short 921K.*
5. **ADD.** With a chat log attached, a chat spike forces its moment in (`livechat.py` exists; make it a hard keep). *77 "watched live" comments; 8 complaints about missing scenes.*
6. **KEEP.** Driving, looting, scoping with no kill, crate spins and lobby chat with no punchline rank lowest and get cut. *All 12 flattest runs (47-203 s).*

### B. Highlight opening

7. **CHANGE.** No Chicken Dinner banner or final kill in the cold open. Replace that beat with the best mid-match fight or the best line with its reply; end on a laugh or knock call. Keep the hook text "… + CHICKEN DINNER 🏆". *0 of 93 open on their WWCD; withheld results end5 1.84 vs 0.92.*
8. **CHANGE.** Cold open 12-20 s (now 15-25 s), never over 30 s. *Skip spikes at story start in 9 of 16 videos starting at 21-35 s.*

10. **ADD.** First beat by style: a fight with comms (skill streams) or the funniest line with a subtitle (talk streams). *Fight-first p2 2.18 on Goblin; line-first f10 1.73.*
11. **CHANGE.** Hard cuts between beats; at most one whoosh, at the teaser-to-match hand-off. *Teasers measured switch by hard cuts; sound unheard, low confidence.*
12. **KEEP.** "IN THIS VIDEO" plus hook title; every beat reappears in full later. *Joker and Goblin hero edits.*

## 9. What could not be measured, and how sure each rule is

**Nothing was heard**: laughs, shouts and music are known only from caption tags and comments, so whooshes and music beats could not be checked. **The heatmap is relative and coarse**: scaled 0-1 per video, mixing skips with replays, in 1% bins, and only on videos above about 50K views (80K for Shorts). Retention, click-through and swipe-away rates are private, so no rule is proven to raise views. **Labels are one person's judgement**, with no second rater. **Era and channel confound comparisons**, and views are a one-day snapshot. **Frame timing rests on few examples** (one word-level punch-in). Recent low-view uploads are thin. None of these rules has been tested on a Kaatclip upload; YouTube Studio retention on Kaatclip's first real uploads is the only way to measure their effect.

| Rule group (section 8) | Confidence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing on kills; fights as one take (14, 23) | High | 18 of 18 videos, no exceptions |
| Talk after fights kept; talk scored as high as kills (2, 15) | High | 393 quoted moments, heatmap agreement, three channels |
| Frame 0 voice or gunfire; no slow start (9, 13, 38) | High | Opening tables, the 7 below-mean openings, Shorts dark-frame split |
| End within seconds of the payoff; no cards or dead tails (31-34, 39) | High | 518 videos with late climaxes; Shorts tail split |
| No Dinner in the cold open (7) | High | 0 of 93 open on the result; end5 1.84 against 0.92 |
| Shorts ranked by people and reactions (35) | High for reach, medium for causation | All 11 big hits are person moments, but feed and era confound |
| Cold open 12-20 s (8) | Medium | Skip signature from 38 videos; threshold inferred |
| Cut dead holds; pacing check (16, 19, 20) | Medium | Flat-run reading in 12 videos; exact limits are design choices |
| Reaction cutaway, black-and-white despair face (26, 27) | Medium | Peaks on several examples, from LoLzZz's multi-camera edits |
| People-first titles; reaction thumbnails (46-48) | Medium | Strong comment and view signals; thumbnails not tested for click-through |
| Shorts 15-60 s (37) | Medium | No clean length effect either way |
| Hard cuts instead of whooshes; no banner push-in (11, 28) | Low-medium | Visual evidence only; sound unheard |
| Chat-spike keeps (5) | Medium | Clear viewer complaint, but only applies when a chat log exists |
| Optional stickers, story stitch, question titles (30, 44, 49) | Low | One to a few examples each |

## Conclusion

The six channels differ in style, but their viewers want the same thing: **a moment that pays off, with a person reacting to it, and nothing slow around it**. That changes what Kaatclip's ranker is for. The old instinct, to find kills and make them louder, is the one thing no editor in 18 videos does. It also misses half of what viewers point at. The better model is a ranker of *reactions*: the push before the kill, the shout after it, the teammate's reply, the line that becomes the comment. The highest-value detail Kaatclip currently risks losing is the few seconds right after a fight, and the next gain is moving the Dinner out of the cold open so the ending carries the video.

The second insight is that Kaatclip's two products need two different rankings. The 16:9 highlight is watched by fans, often the live audience coming back, who skim for skill and payoffs and check that their remembered moments are there.
