# Each streamer cuts one match differently

All three streamers build their long highlights around **one match or one slice of a session, told in order**, but each one keeps a different share of it, by different rules. Goblin's stream cuts keep **23-31% of a match** fight by fight and keep the final fight whole. LoLzZz keeps **about 45% of a record match**, cutting the middle hardest and the end zones least. JokerClips pulls **15-30 short reaction units (median 13 s)** out of 30-160 minutes of a stream and pads them to exactly 8:00-8:10. The deeper study corrects three things in the earlier report. Goblin's main-channel highlights are **not cut from his streams at all**; they come from clean phone recordings, and the stream cuts go to his second channel. JokerClips is run by Joker's own editor and works in **two modes**: a single match kept nearly whole, or a two-stream compilation joined by a "THE NEXT DAY…" card that often jumps backwards in time. LoLzZz does **not** keep every fight; he cuts some kills, and the cut is uneven across the match. For Kaatclip, which edits a creator's own PC/OBS stream recording, the closest models are Goblin's second-channel stream cut (esports), LoLzZz's record cut (challenge) and JokerClips' session cut (comedy). Each can be written as rules over a match map Kaatclip does not yet build: match boundaries, fight windows, result screens and comedy units. Most lead-in and lead-out seconds were measured to only ±2-5 s from public captions and preview frames, so the owner's own test streams should set the final numbers.

All measurements below were taken on 12 Nov 2026 from public YouTube data only. The method aligned each highlight's Hindi auto-captions word by word against its source stream's captions, read YouTube's preview storyboards (one frame every 1-10 s) and used the "most replayed" heatmap. No video or audio was downloaded. Fifteen Goblin, six Joker and six LoLzZz highlights were mapped to their source streams.

## Six channels, two pipelines per creator

Every creator splits the work between a main channel and a second channel, and the split always puts the volume of stream cuts on the second one. **Goblin** streams scrims on YouTube 0-6 times a month (30 streams from Dec 2025 to Nov 2026), usually starting at 19:00-21:00 IST (median 4.45 h) ([streams](https://www.youtube.com/@goblinbgmi/streams)). His main channel posts about one curated phone-recorded match highlight a month (median 381 s), clustered after tournaments. @goblinsh0rts carries the stream cuts, LAN broadcast cuts, TDM/WOW custom rooms, funny edits, vlogs and about 5 Shorts a month. Recent Shorts credit outside fan editors ("Edit by - instagram…", [3gtSMtb1osg](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/3gtSMtb1osg)). **Joker** streams about 14.5 times a month (median 5 h, starting 20:30-22:15 IST) on @Jokerkihavelii, which otherwise holds vlogs and lifestyle Shorts. Every edited BGMI highlight lives on JokerClips, which has 659 videos and 152 Shorts, and Shorts have become rare there since Feb 2026 ([@jokerclipss/shorts](https://www.youtube.com/@jokerclipss/shorts)). **LoLzZz** streams 15-28 times a month, 5.2-6.8 h each, starting 21:05-21:52 IST ([streams](https://www.youtube.com/@LoLzZzGaming/streams)). The main channel got 16 edited long videos in Jan-Oct 2026, almost all record matches. LoLzZz Plays carries second records, cash challenges, "vs streamer" fights, reactions and about 2 Shorts a week ([@lolzzzplays](https://www.youtube.com/@lolzzzplays/videos)).

Highlights are rare relative to streaming. Goblin's second channel turns a strong stream into two or three single-match videos, and many streams into none. Joker's 14 streams a month yield 5-10 highlights; one stream can feed two highlights from separate time ranges ([X_QBQqfO-9g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_QBQqfO-9g) fed both [TIDXJ1YeirM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIDXJ1YeirM) and [3c0nLXL8k6U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3c0nLXL8k6U), with no overlap). LoLzZz gets about one main highlight per 6-12 streams, and his stream titles show the edit waiting for the goal ("60 SOLO KILLS KAB HONGE?" → "70 SOLO KILLS TODAY?"). The packaging is minimal everywhere: **no chapters on any channel**, empty or boilerplate descriptions, and the effort goes into the title and the thumbnail.

Three patterns run across the table. The first is that **the keep ratio tracks what the video promises**. Goblin's stream cut promises "how we won (or nearly won)", so it keeps only fights and throws away three-quarters of the match. LoLzZz promises "how I got to 72", so he keeps nearly half the match to make the count believable. He still drops the second quarter's bot kills and driving to as little as 9%, which is where the earlier "every fight stays" rule was wrong. Joker promises eight minutes of laughs, so the gameplay is barely a frame. The second is that **lead-in and lead-out scale with what the viewer needs to understand**. A fight needs the call that started it (0-16 s), a record needs the call-outs before each kill (11-28 s), and a joke needs only its trigger (2-10 s) and must end within 3 s of the punchline. The third is that **no one adds a kill counter, chapters or match cards to a single-match video**. The game's own screens (kill feed, WWCD, MVP, results table) and the streamer's spoken count carry the stats, and the editors spend their graphics on faces, memes and teasers instead.

The heatmaps show where the edit's value lands. Goblin's main highlights peak on the final fight (for example 459-469 s of [lBswYFSS_rQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lBswYFSS_rQ) and 332-343 s of [ml5FitUoqf8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ml5FitUoqf8), just before the WWCD). LoLzZz's peak more often in the middle third, on a squad wipe or a labelled meme (5:22 of [EJTupVtHuY8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EJTupVtHuY8) is the "IGL / LOLZZZ" meme). In Joker's videos the last heatmap bin falls to about zero every time, so a comedy ending only needs to stop cleanly. The cold open matters in all three: in LoLzZz's videos the opening bin sits above the video's mean in all 13 with a heatmap, at 1.03-4.55 times the mean.

## Rules for Kaatclip's automatic editor, style by style

The rules below turn the measurements into instructions. **(M)** marks a number measured in this study. **(D)** marks a design choice: consistent with the data, but not observed directly, and to be tuned on the owner's test streams. The owner's decisions are already applied: the long highlight is 16:9, its length is whatever the match needs, and Shorts are cut from the highlight plus a same-day teaser.

### Challenge style (LoLzZz model): one record match at about 45%

1. **Unit.** One match per highlight, chosen by its final kill count (M: every main-channel record is a single match; "100 in 3 matches" is the multi-match exception). Make a highlight when the match beats the creator's stored personal best or clears a goal stated in the stream title or speech, such as "mera target 70 hai" (M: the titles and cold opens state the goal). For creators without records, take the session's top-kill match and offer it only above a kill threshold the creator sets (D).
2. **Second strong match.** When a session has two qualifying matches, the better one is the main highlight and the other is offered as a secondary cut, for example for a second channel (M: the 8 Sep 2026 stream gave 51 to Plays and 55 to the main channel). Do not post two near-identical numbers on one channel within a few days (D, from views: the 55 posted a day after the 51 drew the fewest views of the 2026 records, 360,625).
3. **Entry.** Start at the landing, or keep a 5-20 s bridge of drop map plus parachute (M). Keep a lobby donation or challenge read if one exists, as stakes (M: the 61-kill video keeps "a token for you to make 51 kills").
4. **Keep ratio.** Keep 0.41-0.52 of the match, median 0.45 (M). Shape it by quarter of match time: about 0.4 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.65 (M, median of six). A 25-30 minute match therefore lands at 11-14 minutes (M).
5. **Fight pieces.** Pieces of 10-80 s, allowed up to about 110 s for a continuous fight (M). Start 10-30 s before the first kill or knock of a fight, on the call-outs (weakly M: median 11-28 s). End 5-10 s after the last kill (weakly M: 2-12 s). Merge fights less than about 20 s apart (D).
6. **What goes.** Hard-cut, without cards: bot kills, looting, driving with no enemies, route talk, chat with visitors or viewers, recall waits and post-match plugs (M: the contents of the 250 s and 192 s cuts in the 72-kill match). Real kills may go when they sit inside a dead stretch (M). Keep the streamer's spoken count lines ("35 kill aur chahiye") whenever they fall inside kept pieces (M), because they replace an on-screen counter.
7. **Cold open (29-69 s, median about 39 s; M).** In order: 0.5-1 s of black; a full-screen facecam crop stating the day's goal, taken from the first 15 minutes of the stream or from the previous day's stream (M: lines from 0:02:45-0:14:27, and the 72-kill open borrows from the day before); 2-3 teaser kills of 3-5 s each against facecam reactions; the final number or catchphrase from the end of the match over music; an optional flash of the MVP screen as a spoiler (M: the 61 open); 0.5-1 s of black.
8. **Inserts.** In the first half, one insert every 60-120 s: a 2-5 s facecam punch-in right after a multi-kill, or a 2-4 s labelled cutaway (M: "ENEMY :", "IGL", squad names). At most 1-2 colour-bar or whip transitions per video (M). Kaatclip uses its own stickers and labels in place of film or TV meme clips (D: the meme clips carry the copyright risk).
9. **Ending (40-75 s after the record kill; M).** A zoom or punch-in on the final kill, 5-20 s of facecam celebration with the catchphrase, the WWCD screen (3-10 s), the MVP screen showing the kill count (5-15 s), a short facecam reflection, then 5-10 s of black for end-screen elements. Cut everything after.
10. **Multi-match challenge.** Use every match of the challenge, in order, at about 5.4-8.4 minutes each (M). The results screens and the spoken checkpoints ("32 kill hue to 68 aur") act as the transitions; no "Match 2" cards (M).
11. **Frame.** The stream layout at 16:9, unchanged (M). No added kill counter and no chapters (M).
12. **Packaging and timing.** Title: "I MADE A (NEW) WORLD RECORD OF {N} SOLO KILLS (IN {mode/update}) | {creator tag}" (M). Thumbnail: a reused creator photo, a rounded gameplay card, "{N} SOLO KILLS" in yellow over "WORLD RECORD!!" in white, one shocked emoji (M). Flag "new personal best" as the hook (D, from views: the 61 and the 72 beat the 62 and 64 that followed similar numbers). Suggest posting at 12:00 IST the day after the match (M).
13. **Shorts.** A 20-30 s vertical teaser built from the last 20 s (final kills → WWCD → MVP with the number), a one-line ironic top caption, gameplay crop on top (about 60%) and facecam below (about 40%), and a 3-4 s "FULL VIDEO ON YOUTUBE" end card (M: [FyHDoGMnHO4](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/FyHDoGMnHO4), posted 5 h after the long video). Further Shorts: the mid-match squad wipe that the heatmap favours (D).

### Esports style (Goblin model): every fight of the match worth telling

14. **Unit and selection.** One match per video (M). Score each match on: WWCD, then a #2 or #3 with a long final fight; total team and solo kills; a 1vN clutch; a named teammate or opponent; comms intensity in the last circle (D, consistent with what was kept). A strong session yields 2-3 videos (M: 21 Feb 2026). For TDM, WOW and other custom rooms, the whole room session is the unit (M: 15-25 min spans).
15. **Keep ratio and shape.** Keep 23-31% of a classic match (M) in 4-11 stretches (M). Mid-match fights run 25-87 s (M); the final fight runs from the final circle through the result screen, **whole**, 142-162 s observed (M). Custom rooms and Ultimate Royale keep 43-48%, in stretches of 2-44 s (M).
16. **Order.** Chronological (M in every classic-match map).
17. **Fight windows.** Group knocks and kills into a fight when they are less than 25-45 s apart (D; the shortest real gaps between kept stretches were 19-57 s, M). Start each stretch 8-15 s before the first shot or knock, snapped back to the start of the comms phrase that calls the enemy (D default; M: real starts sat 0-16 s before contact, on calls like "इधर से गाड़ी आ रहा है"). End 5-20 s after the last finish, snapped to the end of the confirmation or banter phrase (M approx). Merge stretches less than about 20 s apart (M: one case at 19 s).
18. **Bridges.** At most one 10-30 s bridge (a drive with comms, a zone call) where the rotation changes the story (D; M: one example, "पूरा जोन… ब्लू साइड").
19. **What goes.** The lobby, inventory and loadout, between-match chat (10-21 min per match, M); the plane; landing loot until first contact (2-6 min, M); looting, driving or scoping with no knock or kill within about 20 s (D threshold; M: gaps of 19 s to 11 min were removed); **spectating after death** (M: never shown); results tables beyond about 5 s (D).
20. **Ending.** The result screen (WWCD or #N), then 15-30 s of comms or a facecam reaction (M). A facecam reaction that gives the video its title earns its place ("teammates shocked", M: [5zW1ag4_d_o](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zW1ag4_d_o)).
21. **Quick-cut variant (default for a stream recording).** The stream layout as is, sponsor banner and facecam included; no added graphics; no cold open (M: 3 of 4 cuts), or an optional 15-25 s cold open from a top fight that the body cut out (M: one case). Typical output 4.5-7.5 minutes (M: 279-448 s).
22. **Hero variant.** A 15-25 s teaser recycled from later fights, usually the final fight, under a short label ("in this video -"), then a 3-5 s black or logo card, then 3-5 s of parachute, then the body (M: 6 of 9 main videos). Optional graphics: a branded side panel when the source is narrower than 16:9 (M); a face sticker plus subtitle on at most 1-2 funny lines (M: one video); a 2-4 s zoom on the WWCD banner (M). Typical output 8-9.5 minutes (M: 486-553 s). The clean, facecam-free frame of Goblin's hero edits needs a clean game recording; a stream recording with an overlay can only approximate it by cropping (D).
23. **Audio.** Comms at full level throughout (M: 61-85% of seconds with speech). Music only under the teaser and speech-free bridges (M).
24. **No running kill counter, no chapters, no match card** in a single-match video (M across 29 storyboarded videos). A map or "MATCH 2" card only for multi-match tournament days (M).
25. **Packaging and timing.** Offer two title templates: "Solo {k} Finishes WWCD | {team_k} Finishes | {event}" and "{creator} + {teammate} {moment}" (M). Default to the people-and-moment title when a teammate or reaction is detected (D, from views: 112K against 25K on the same stream). Description: hashtags only (M). Suggest posting the next day at 12:00 or 21:00 IST (M).
26. **Shorts.** One per 1vN clutch or best banter beat: 20-40 s, starting 3-5 s before the first knock, gameplay crop over facecam (about 60/40), ending on a meme sting such as a B&W freeze or skull sticker (M: 20 recent Shorts, median 32.5 s).

### Comedy style (JokerClips model): reaction units fitted to eight minutes

27. **Source window.** The first 30-120 minutes of the BGMI part of a stream (M: later hours contribute little or nothing). Exclude other games on the same stream (M).
28. **Unit construction.** A unit runs from its trigger (a death, a knock, a question, the first line of a roast) to the punchline: start 2-10 s before the punchline, end 0-3 s after the last laughing or punchline line, and drop the next callout (M on 3 hand-read units). Keep roast monologues whole up to about 46 s (M). Inside one continuous fight, allow up to about 110 s with internal silences over 5 s trimmed (M: 109 s max; D: the 5 s trim).
29. **Unit statistics to aim for.** Median about 13 s, IQR 7-19 s (M). Merge units less than 30 s apart into one block with micro-trims (M: 39 of 93 forward gaps were 30 s or less). About 15-30 units per video (D, from the mapped videos).
30. **Order.** Stream order (M: 93 forward jumps, 11 back). Allowed exceptions: one hook unit, the best moment of the session, at 0:00-0:15; a short pre-match chat bit as the ending; local swaps under 5 minutes (M).
31. **Two modes.** *Single-match mode*: when one match is dense with comedy, keep it almost whole (M: 9.6 min → 7.7 min), trim dead seconds, add no teaser or cards, end on the VICTORY or results screen. The trigger is speech on more than about 80% of seconds and at least 6 laugh or shout bursts (D). *Compilation mode*: when one stream yields too little (D: under about 6 minutes of good units), append the best block of another stream from the last three weeks behind a 4-6 s "THE NEXT DAY…" card, newer or better stream first (M: 3 of 6 mapped highlights; material used within about 3 weeks). Use "2 SECONDS LATER…" style cards for jumps within a match (M).
32. **Teaser.** Compilation mode only: "IN THIS VIDEO" over 3-5 cuts of 3-5 s, 10-22 s in total, taken from the body's best punchlines (M).
33. **Look.** The stream layout at 16:9 with its overlays (M). On every talk unit, crop-zoom to the facecam region (M: 25-45% of frames in 2026); place two webcams side by side when a co-streamer's cam is present (M). A visual change every 5-10 s (M approx). One burned-in subtitle per unit for the key line (M). 3-6 labelled inserts of 1-3 s per video ("CHAT", the teammate's name), using Kaatclip's own stickers in place of film clips (M: the count; D: the stickers). Keep results and MVP screens when they land in a unit (M).
34. **Speech floor.** At least 80% of output seconds with speech (D; M: the channel runs 77-94%).
35. **Length.** 480-495 s (M: 69 of 133 at 480-489 s). Go to 600-700 s only when the source is exceptionally rich (M: two such videos).
36. **Ending.** Stop on the final punchline or a facecam reaction, or a "SUBSCRIBE" card of 10 s or less (M). Never end on a long quiet fight (D; M: the last heatmap bin is near zero in every video).
37. **No reuse.** Each unit appears in one highlight only (M). Leftover top units wait in a pool for a "part 2" within three weeks (D, from M).
38. **Packaging.** Title: "[Streamer] & [Teammate] *[CONFLICT/NOVELTY WORD]*😂 *FUNNY BGMI HIGHLIGHT!*" or "[Streamer] Being [Streamer] For 8 Minutes" (M). Thumbnail: 2-4 shouting or laughing facecam crops and a speech bubble quoting the video's own peak line (M: "ZAALIM HAI DUNIYA PAR TU!!" is the 6:00 line of [3c0nLXL8k6U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3c0nLXL8k6U)). Description: a main-channel link plus 3 hashtags (M). Name the co-star: views follow the co-star and the conflict more than the gameplay (M: Snax- and Goldy-era titles reached 0.35-1.06M; Rega-only duo titles sit at 70-130K).
39. **Shorts.** One relationship moment, 18-40 s, payoff in the last 3-5 s, a constant top line promising the ending ("Wait For The End😂"), facecam on top and gameplay or a friend's cam below, and an optional 2-3 s "watch full video" end card (M: [_RgXLRs1wx8](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/_RgXLRs1wx8), [SATVDilBDOY](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/SATVDilBDOY), [tb7vUafHeso](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/tb7vUafHeso)). Kill Shorts do far worse on this channel (M: [icIr3BWd1nM](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/icIr3BWd1nM), 145K).

### Mixed style (design only): a kill match with comedy garnish

40. No channel studied runs a separate "mixed" format, but two of them already blend. Goblin's hero edit drops face-sticker subtitles on teammates' funny lines into a match (M: [-vFxWHPrv2s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-vFxWHPrv2s)), and LoLzZz garnishes a record match with labelled cutaways and facecam punch-ins every 1-2 minutes (M). So the mixed style is best defined as **the challenge or esports cut of one match, plus comedy units from inside that same match**: talk units built by rule 28, a facecam zoom on each, at most one per 60-120 s, and never pulled from another match or stream (D). The ending follows the kill style (result screen plus reaction), and the title follows the people-and-moment template (D).

### Rules shared by every style

41. **Teaser Short, same day.** Only LoLzZz does this (M: 5 h after the long video, from the last 20 s); Goblin's and Joker's Shorts are not systematically cut from their highlights (M). Kaatclip's same-day teaser therefore follows the LoLzZz model: proof or payoff from the end of the highlight, a top caption, and a "full video" end card, posted a few hours after the long video (D, from M).
42. **Shorts from the highlight's best moments.** For kill styles: the top clutch or squad wipe, 20-40 s, stacked gameplay over facecam, a meme sting at the payoff (M: Goblin and LoLzZz Shorts). For comedy: the top relationship moment as in rule 39 (M).
43. **Never show a dead stretch in full.** Lobby, plane, looting with no contact, waiting and spectating are cut in every style (M).
44. **Keep the game's own proof screens** (WWCD, #N placement, MVP, results table) rather than drawing counters (M across all three channels).

## What the engine must detect, and where it will trip

Kaatclip already reads most of the raw signals these rules need: the kill feed and knocks, the players-left counter, the Chicken Dinner banner and accolades, a Hinglish transcript with word timings, YAMNet laughter and shouting scores, and the creator's moment markers. What the rules add is **structure over those signals**: a match map, fight windows and result screens for the kill styles, and comedy units with triggers and punchlines for the comedy style. The table lists each detection, which styles depend on it, and the pitfalls the study exposed.

| Detection | Needed by | Built from | Pitfall seen in the study |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Match boundaries** (lobby → plane → landing → result → next lobby) | All kill styles; comedy single-match mode | Players-left reset, the result screen, the lobby returning | **Recall/respawn event modes** put a second parachute mid-match (M: [0inNw0cj1p0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0inNw0cj1p0), [ckdrrSD2Y0w](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ckdrrSD2Y0w), [Gn1AvcFXl-Y](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gn1AvcFXl-Y)), so a parachute or a death does not end a match. Only the result screen or a fresh lobby does |
| **Mode** (classic, TDM, WOW, Gun Game, Ultimate Royale, event modes) | All | No plane or 100-player counter in arenas; round structure | Custom rooms need a different recipe (M: 43-48% keep, stretches of 2-44 s, respawn gaps of 8-60 s) |
| **Own kills vs teammates' kills** in the kill feed | Challenge (the count), esports titles | Kill-feed names; the MVP and results screens for the final number | The squad's kills also run through the feed; the solo count must come from the creator's own name, cross-checked against the MVP screen |
| **Bot kills** | Challenge | No enemy call-outs, very fast kills early in the match | LoLzZz cuts them (M: "saare bot maar liye" inside a removed span); the HUD alone does not mark a bot |
| **Fight windows** (knocks and kills grouped, with lead-in and lead-out) | Esports, challenge, mixed | Kill-feed times; snapping to transcript phrase edges | Lead-in seconds rest on comms: the stretch should open on the enemy call. Music-free silent fights have no phrase to snap to |
| **Final circle** | Esports (keep whole) | Players left ≤ about 16, or the last zone | A #2 finish can have a long final fight with no WWCD (M: [wDgeZBn80VM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wDgeZBn80VM), 162 s kept) |
| **Result screens**: WWCD banner, #N placement, MVP with the kill count, results table | All kill styles; comedy keeps them when funny | OCR of the end screens | The MVP screen carries the authoritative kill number for titles and thumbnails |
| **Spectating after death** | Esports (cut), challenge (cut) | "Spectating" label, the view switching to a teammate | In recall modes the player comes back; in classic squads, watching a teammate win is dead time unless the creator is reacting |
| **Goal line and catchphrase** | Challenge cold open | Stream title ("70 SOLO KILLS TODAY?"), transcript ("mera target 70 hai", "record tere bhai ka") | The goal is often said hours before the match, or on the previous day's stream (M); search the first 15 minutes and the previous stream |
| **Spoken count lines** | Challenge | Transcript ("35 kill aur chahiye", "saat kill aur") | They must survive the cut; a cut that drops them loses the running count |
| **Facecam rectangle** | Comedy zoom, challenge punch-ins, all Shorts | The existing facecam finder | Layouts carry other overlays (follower and subscriber counters, donation QR codes, sponsor bars) that the crop must avoid or accept |
| **Comedy units**: trigger → punchline → end | Comedy, mixed | YAMNet laughter and shouting, words per minute, death or knock triggers, Hinglish cues ("naseeb kharab", "GG", "kisne", teammate names, repeated words), questions | Speech overlaps between several voices; the auto-transcript has no speaker labels, so "teammate's roast" vs "own line" cannot be told apart yet |
| **Streamer's clip flags** | Comedy (strong marker) | Transcript: "clip kaat", "editor", "highlight", "ye clip banega" | Joker uses these partly as a running joke (M), so a flag adds score but does not force a cut |
| **BGMI segments of a mixed-game stream** | Comedy | Game detection over time | Joker's streams switch to Valorant, GTA RP and chat (M); those parts are never used |
| **Second creator on call** | Comedy (side-by-side cams) | Two faces in the frame | Joker's editor also cuts to **co-streamers' own streams** (M: "REGALTOS" overlay in [3c0nLXL8k6U](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3c0nLXL8k6U)), and LoLzZz once used **a teammate's stream as the whole source** (M: [3i83F-jSotA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3i83F-jSotA)). Kaatclip has only the creator's own recording, so other people's POV is out of reach and out of policy |
| **Personal best per creator** | Challenge selection and titles | Stored history of match kill counts | Needs a per-creator record across jobs; the first jobs have no history |

Three limits of the evidence matter for these detections. First, the lead-in and lead-out seconds come from caption alignment (±2-3 s) and preview frames 5-10 s apart, and the LoLzZz lead-in rests on only 2-9 pieces per video, because his fights often sit under music. Second, the number of mapped matches is small: three classic Goblin matches, six LoLzZz records and six Joker highlights. Third, the earlier study found that Kaatclip's kill feed is unreadable on some test layouts (half-transparent rows, busy overlays), so fight windows and clutches will be best-effort on those streams. These numbers should be tuned on the owner's own full-length streams.

## Conclusion

The deeper study shows that the "style" of a BGMI highlight is mostly **a keep ratio and a pair of lead-in and lead-out numbers attached to a promise**. A fight story keeps a quarter of the match and opens on the enemy call. A record keeps nearly half and spends 40 s proving the number at both ends. A comedy cut keeps a few percent of the session, opens two seconds before the trigger and closes within three seconds of the laugh. All three run on the same match map, and none of them needs a counter, chapters or a card that the game does not already draw. That makes the hard part of Kaatclip's editor detection, not rendering. It has to know where a match really ends (recall modes), whose kill it was, which kills were bots, and where a joke starts and stops.

The study also changes how the two-channel habit should be read. Every creator studied keeps a polished, slower channel and a fast second channel of stream cuts, and Kaatclip's natural place is the fast one. The fast channel is where cuts go up within a day, the overlay stays, graphics are minimal, and the rules above can be followed almost to the second. Goblin's polished phone-recorded edits and Joker's hand-placed meme jokes sit beyond what one stream recording can supply. A Kaatclip that matches the second channels' timing and cut rules would already be doing the work these creators pay editors or fans to do.
