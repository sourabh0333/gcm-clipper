# Games Indian creators stream and clip, and how hard each is to auto-clip (as of Nov 2026)

Research date: 4 Nov 2026. About 18 searches and fetches. Dates are given for every figure. No India-only "hours watched by game" ranking was found from Stream Hatchet or Streams Charts in free sources. The ranking below combines global YouTube Gaming data, Indian esports peaks, Sensor Tower MAU and which games the top Indian creators play.

## Q1. Which games dominate Indian YouTube Live and short-form gaming content in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
Free Fire (MAX, plus the new Free Fire India launched on 28 Oct 2026) and BGMI are the two mobile titles that anchor Indian gaming creators. Free Fire has about 3.7x BGMI's monthly users in India. GTA V (story and roleplay), Minecraft and horror/party games lead the PC side. Valorant, Roblox and Fortnite are smaller but growing. India produced the third-most YouTube Gaming watch hours of any country in 2025.

### Inferences
- Suggested order for expanding beyond BGMI, by Indian audience size: **1. Free Fire MAX / Free Fire India** (largest MAU, a huge mobile creator base, a new installed base from the Oct 2026 relaunch, and the same battle-royale genre as BGMI); **2. GTA V, story and RP** (big Hindi creators and long dialogue-heavy streams, which are ideal for clipping); **3. Minecraft** (SMP series, growing fast worldwide); **4. Valorant** (a growing PC creator base, a standard HUD, existing open tools); **5. Horror/party games and Roblox** (a variety category with no single HUD). Lower priority: COD Mobile, Pokemon Unite, Clash games, New State, Fortnite.
- Free Fire is the obvious next game. It is mobile like BGMI, has kill feeds and a win banner, and its player base just grew. The 6-hour playtime cap only limits how long people play, so streams will still be long.
- Long-tail creators (the product's likely buyers) mirror these top creators. That makes BGMI and Free Fire the volume market among small streamers, while GTA RP and Minecraft creators skew toward PC.

## Q2. For each top game, what are the clip-worthy moments, which on-screen signals reveal them, and is detection mostly visual, audio, or both?

### Takeaway
Shooters and battle royales (BGMI, Free Fire, Valorant, COD Mobile) can be detected visually from fixed HUD elements: the kill feed, a centre-screen kill banner, the elimination counter and win/loss banners, backed up by audio spikes. GTA RP, Minecraft and horror/party content have no reliable HUD event, so they need speech, laughter and loudness detection plus an LLM judge on the transcript. That is the path the project already uses for funny moments.

### Cited Findings
- **Crispy** (MIT licence, 158 stars) detects highlights with a neural network for Valorant, Overwatch 2 and CS2. It uses image recognition on kill indicators for League of Legends, and image recognition plus OCR on the kill feed for The Finals; the README notes OCR is slow — [GitHub Flowtter/crispy](https://github.com/Flowtter/crispy)
- **montage_maker** detects kills by OCR on the game's kill log, filters for the player's gamertag as killer, and builds TikTok-style montages; OCR makes long clips take minutes — [GitHub rlmsinclair/montage_maker](https://github.com/rlmsinclair/montage_maker)
- **vidcom** uses YOLO with a screen region per game. For Valorant it watches the centre kill-confirmation area rather than the kill feed — [GitHub XAOSTECH/vidcom](https://ithub.global.ssl.fastly.net/XAOSTECH/vidcom)
- A personal write-up on training a YOLOv8 Valorant killfeed detector reports 400+ labelled frames and 95.96% accuracy on unseen clips (self-reported) — [Notion write-up](https://languid-sheet-e9c.notion.site/Valorant-Killfeed-Detection-Model-v0-to-killfeed_v5-1d64d388895680778667c65f3e9daa1e)
- EZClips (CS2) uses template matching on the kill-feed icon — [GitHub corp14x3/EZClips](https://github.com/corp14x3/EZClips)
- PlayPulse combines YOLOv8 and EasyOCR to pull events from Valorant footage and build highlights — [ecosyste.ms listing](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fsrp4214%2Fplaypulse_player_tracking)

### Inferences
These are from general knowledge of the games' HUDs; they are not sourced and should be checked against real VODs before building.
- **BGMI** (current): clip moments are kills, knock-downs, multi-kills, chicken dinners and clutch final circles. Signals are the kill feed at top-left/centre, a centre "You eliminated X" message, the kill count at top-right, and the "Winner Winner Chicken Dinner" banner. The HUD is fixed per game, but streamers add webcam and overlay layouts, and phone aspect ratios move it. Detection: visual first (OCR or a small detector on banners), plus audio loudness and speech.
- **Free Fire MAX / India**: clip moments are kills, squad wipes, "Booyah!" wins and headshot streaks. Signals are the kill notifications, the elimination counter and the Booyah banner. The HUD is similar to BGMI (mobile, fixed per game version), so the BGMI approach should carry over with new templates or a retrained detector. Detection: visual plus audio. Effort: low to medium.
- **GTA V story/RP**: clip moments are funny dialogue, chases, police scenes and fails. There is no game-event HUD for RP, and servers add custom HUDs. Detection is driven by audio and speech: Hinglish transcription, laughter, loudness, and an LLM judge. The project's funny-moment pipeline covers this; effort is medium and sits mostly in the AI judge.
- **Minecraft**: clip moments are builds, deaths, PvP, funny SMP dialogue and jump scares. Death screens ("You died!") and chat messages can be read by OCR, but most value is in commentary. Detection: mostly audio and speech. Effort: medium.
- **Valorant** (PC): clip moments are multi-kills, aces, clutches and round wins. It has a highly standard HUD (kill feed at top-right, a centre kill banner with kill count, round result banners), and open tools already exist (Crispy, MIT). Effort: low.
- **Horror/party games and Roblox**: clip moments are jump-scares and screams. There is no common HUD, so detection is audio only (scream and loudness spikes plus transcript). Effort: low to medium with the current audio stack.

### Gaps
- No public, India-specific study of how much BGMI and Free Fire streamers' HUDs and overlays vary was found.
- I did not check whether Crispy is still maintained, as its last-commit date was not visible.

## Q3. Are there public datasets or open models for event detection per game, and what are their licences?

### Takeaway
Datasets exist but are small and scattered. Free Fire kill-feed sets on Roboflow Universe are tiny (one has 20 images); there are several CC BY 4.0 kill-feed sets and a multi-game set of about 1k images. Valorant has the most open tooling. Expect to label your own data, about 400 or more frames per game based on the Valorant write-up.

### Cited Findings
- Roboflow Universe "free-fire-killfeeds" (avs-workplace): version 1 has 20 images and a YOLOv11-nano model; the licence was not shown in the snippet — [Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/avs-workplace/free-fire-killfeeds)
- "freefire-ai" (avs-workplace): CC BY 4.0, contents not described — [Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/avs-workplace/freefire-ai)
- "kill_feed" (s-workspace-kpodn): CC BY 4.0; the game is not stated, and it also has a "tower" class — [Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/s-workspace-kpodn/kill_feed/model/2/train/results)
- "stg-kill-message" (ELKROOM): multi-game, about 1k images, includes a "freefire-medal" class — [Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/elkroom-ojb92/stg-kill-message)
- "Valorant kill banner" project — [Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/suman-kumar-dx18l/valorant-kill-banner); general "killfeed-tldkp" — [Roboflow](https://universe.roboflow.com/setu-it3tm/killfeed-tldkp)
- Crispy is MIT-licensed (Valorant, CS2, OW2, LoL, The Finals) — [GitHub](https://github.com/Flowtter/crispy)

### Inferences
- CC BY 4.0 allows commercial use with attribution, so those sets are usable for a paid product. Check each project page, since the licence was not visible on all of them.
- For Free Fire, the cheapest route is likely to auto-label frames from the owner's own test streams (OCR on banners, then train a YOLO-nano) rather than rely on the 20-image public set.

### Gaps
- No public BGMI, COD Mobile or GTA RP event datasets were confirmed in this search; COD Mobile was not searched directly.
- Image counts and licences of the Roboflow sets were seen only in search snippets, not opened one by one.

## Q4. Which games have legal/IP or platform issues for clipping and monetising?

### Takeaway
Riot (Valorant) and Mojang (Minecraft) explicitly allow ad-monetised gameplay videos, with an "add your own content" condition. Rockstar's policy covers only non-commercial use, though GTA monetisation is widely tolerated in practice. No public creator video policy from Krafton (BGMI) or Garena (Free Fire) was found. A bigger risk across all games is YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule against mass-produced or repetitive uploads, which matters for auto-generated Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- **Riot (Valorant)**: individuals may earn ad revenue from videos and streams, and may take donations or subscriptions if non-subscribers can still watch. Re-uploading or lightly commenting on other people's footage does not count as original. Commercial projects need a written licence — [Riot Legal Jibber Jabber](https://www.riotgames.com/en/legal). Music from Riot's "UGC-friendly" playlist can still attract monetisation claims — [Riot Creator Safe Guidelines](https://www.riotgames.com/en/riot-music-creator-safe-guidelines)
- **Rockstar (GTA V)**: the policy is for "non-commercial" use, defined as not making money from posted footage. It targets spoilers and isolated cutscenes, cheats and unauthorised mods. Take-Two keeps the right to take content down at any time. Last updated 10 Jan 2025 (per search snippet; the fetch of the page timed out) — [Rockstar support](https://support.rockstargames.com/articles/200153756/rockstar-games-video-policy)
- **Mojang (Minecraft)**: lets creators make money from gameplay videos through ads, Twitch and YouTube, if they add enough unique content of their own. Videos must be free to view, and copies cannot be sold. Read via a third-party mirror; the official text was not opened — [ConductAtlas mirror of Minecraft Usage Guidelines](https://conductatlas.com/platform/minecraft/minecraft-usage-guidelines/video-content-monetization-carve-out/)
- **Krafton (BGMI)**: no BGMI-specific creator or video policy was found. Krafton's fan content page found was for Dinkum (June 2025) — [Krafton Dinkum fan content guidelines](https://dinkum.krafton.com/en/policy/fan_content_guidelines)
- **YouTube**: from July 2025 the "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content". Mass-produced, repetitive content, or content with no clear creator contribution, can lose monetisation for the whole channel — [The ATDB](https://www.theatdb.com/news/youtube-clarifies-that-creators-cant-monetize-generic-or-repetitive-content-content-thats-unsatisfying-or-off-putting-or-content-with-fake-ai-experts); [YouTube monetization policies](https://www.youtube.com/intl/en_be/howyoutubeworks/policies/monetization-policies)
- **Regulatory history**: Free Fire was banned in Feb 2022 and BGMI was blocked in 2022 and unbanned in 2023. Creators who depended on one game lost income — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/free-fire-returns-to-india-as-garena-launches-localized-version-four-years-after-ban/); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/bgmi/how-bgmi-s-ban-affected-players-content-creators)

### Inferences
- GCM Clipper clips a creator's own streams for that creator's channel. That is the standard case these policies allow (Riot, Mojang) or tolerate (Rockstar). The product should not offer clipping of other people's streams or esports VODs, which Riot names as not original.
- Supporting several games protects against the ban risk Indian creators have already lived through (Free Fire 2022, BGMI 2022–23).
- Auto-posting many near-identical Shorts risks YouTube's inauthentic-content rule. Captions, commentary-led moments and the creator's own voice help, but this is a product design concern, not a legal one.

### Gaps
- No public Garena (Free Fire) or Activision (COD Mobile) creator video policy was found; Activision's was not searched directly.
- Krafton's BGMI-specific stance on monetised clips is unknown, and no takedown problems for BGMI creators were found either.
- The Mojang text was read through a third-party mirror, not minecraft.net.
