# India gaming-creator market: size, segments, willingness to pay, buyers and distribution (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: this extends `reports/BGMI AI clipper market validation.md`. That report already sizes the BGMI slice (4.12M Indian creators per ISB-Hashfame, ~300k gaming creators per Market.us, ~6,178 Modash-indexed Indian gaming YouTube channels, BCG's 8-10% monetisation rate, 24-30k "earning" gaming creators, editor rates of ₹500-2,000 per Short). Those figures are not repeated as new findings here. Research was desk-only (about 20 searches/fetches); many hard numbers the brief asked for are not published anywhere I could reach, and that is itself a finding.

## 1. How many Indian gaming YouTube channels exist by subscriber tier, and how many stream live regularly?

### Takeaway
No public source (YouTube India, Lumikai, EY-FICCI 2026, Social Blade-style rankings) publishes a count of Indian gaming channels by subscriber tier or a count of regular live streamers. Only the very top is visible: a few dozen Indian gaming channels sit above ~5M subscribers, and the market below 1M must be estimated.

### Cited Findings
- Top of the pyramid (third-party snapshots, figures disagree by source and date): Techno Gamerz ~40-46.7M, Total Gaming ~41-44.8M, AS Gaming ~20.7M, Free Fire India Official ~17.8M, Mythpat and LOKESH GAMER ~16.5M each — [Infidigit](https://www.infidigit.com/blog/top-youtube-gaming-channels/); [Bloom Agency](https://bloomagency.in/top-youtube-gaming-channels/)
- Individual BGMI-era creators: Dynamo Gaming ~10.1M, Jonathan Gaming ~7.6M — [Wikipedia: Dynamo Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamo_Gaming); [Wikipedia: Jonathan Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Gaming)
- A stats roundup says YouTube reported 100M+ channels uploading in India in the past year (as of May 2025) and "more than 15,000" with over 1M subscribers; the source is ambiguous whether 15,000 is India-only or global, so treat as unverified — [Global Media Insight](https://www.globalmediainsight.com/blog/youtube-users-statistics/)
- India Influencer Marketing Report 2025 (GOAT/Kantar, all categories, mainly Instagram): 40.6 lakh influencers, about 30,000 mega or celebrity accounts — [MediaBrief/DeepBrief](https://mediabrief.com/india-influencer-report-2025-goat-kantar-growth/)
- HypeAuditor (Instagram, all categories, page ~mid-2026): 54.3% of Indian influencers are micro-tier; nano accounts have the highest engagement at 5.90% — [HypeAuditor](https://blog.hypeauditor.com/state-of-instagram-influencer-marketing-in-india/)
- FICCI-EY 2026 (released 24 March 2026): India's gamer base was 515-525M in 2025; video gaming revenue projected at ₹92B by 2028 (13% CAGR); no creator counts in summaries — [AnimationXpress](https://www.animationxpress.com/latest-news/ficci-ey-report-2026-indias-gamer-base-crosses-520-million-amid-regulatory-shifts/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/report/ai-digital-shift-reshape-india-me-industry-ficciey-me-report-2026-11258770)
- Lumikai's latest is the State of India Interactive Media Report 2025 (released March 2026): free-to-play gaming $1.5B in CY25 (+17%), gaming time 13 hours/week (+30% YoY), sample ~3,000 mobile users; no creator-tier data surfaced — [Digital in Asia](https://digitalinasia.com/india-gaming-market/); [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/indias-micro-drama-market-hits-300-million-in-year-one-interactive-media-economy-reaches-13-8-billion-lumikai-report/)
- Live viewing is concentrated on YouTube: a 2023 Statista survey (n=3,437) ranked YouTube Live the top live-streaming site in India; StreamHatchet says BGMI audiences and creators are largely on YouTube Live — [Statista](https://statista.com/forecasts/1348444/most-used-video-game-streaming-sites-by-brand-in-india); [StreamHatchet](https://streamhatchet.com/?p=12156)
- Live viewership has softened for some top creators: AFK Gaming data showed BGMI live viewership falling 2021-2023, and BGMI star Scout said live "is not getting that much of a priority" on YouTube — [Digit](https://www.digit.in/news/gaming/bgmi-streaming-popularity-decline-in-india-68213.html); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/bgmi/news-live-streaming-getting-much-priority-bgmi-star-scout-opens-decline-watching-numbers)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE (not sourced; a power-law sketch for planning only): if Modash's ~6,200 India-audience gaming channels approximates channels with meaningful reach (roughly 1k+ engaged subs), a typical creator pyramid would put roughly 100-200 at 1M+, ~1,000-2,000 at 100k-1M, ~5,000-15,000 at 10k-100k, and tens of thousands at 1k-10k (Modash's index likely undercounts small channels). Treat every number here as a hypothesis to replace with a Playboard/Social Blade/vidIQ export.
- The 10k-1M band (a few thousand to ~15k channels) is the realistic first paying segment: big enough to have pain (streams, editor spend), small enough not to have an in-house team.
- Regular live streamers are a subset; with live viewership flattening for top BGMI names, many creators now lead with Shorts and long-form uploads. A clipper must also accept uploaded VODs and recordings, not only live URLs.

### Gaps
- No tier-by-tier count of Indian gaming YouTube channels from YouTube, Playboard, Social Blade, HypeAuditor, Kofluence or Redseer was accessible; this needs a paid tool export (Playboard, vidIQ, HypeAuditor) or a scrape.
- No count of Indian channels that stream live weekly.
- The EY-FICCI 2026 PDF creator section and the full Lumikai 2025 report were not read directly.

## 2. Language split: Hindi/Hinglish vs regional and English

### Takeaway
Hindi/Hinglish dominates the top of Indian gaming YouTube, but Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Malayalam and Marathi gaming channels have grown into multi-million-subscriber businesses. Regional talent agencies now exist, so regional creators are a real secondary segment, not a niche. No source gives a percentage split.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube APAC (2019): Indian gaming content had been mostly Hindi and English; non-Hindi gaming creators under 5,000 subscribers in 2018 hit six-figure counts by late 2019; growth in Marathi, Tamil, Bengali, Telugu and Malayalam gaming noted (dated 2019) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/technology/hungry-indian-online-game-creators-arrive-on-youtube-s-global-map-119111800711_1.html)
- YouTube India (2019-2020): focus on regional languages, especially Tamil, Telugu and Malayalam, plus gaming; Tamil took off first (dated) — [OdishaTV](https://odishatv.in/science/regional-languages-to-drive-creators-in-2020-youtube-india-419905)
- 2026 regional examples: Gaming Tamizhan ~4.1M subscribers, 590M+ views (Tamil); Munna Bhai Gaming ~3.8M, Telugu Gaming FF ~2.5M, Dhanush FF Gamer ~2M (Telugu, mostly Free Fire); Total Gaming streams in Hindi and Bengali — [IWMBuzz, Aug 2026](https://www.iwmbuzz.com/gaming/indias-gaming-a-list-the-players-with-massive-fanbases/2026/08/13); [IndiTubeDb](https://www.inditubedb.com/guides/top-telugu-gaming-youtube-channels-india)
- Skyesports' talent arm Sky Route focuses on South Indian and regional-language creators (roster reportedly includes PVS Gaming, Gaming Tamizhan, Velocity Gaming) and says 200+ creators and teams signed — [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/valorant/news-it-feels-fantastic-our-visions-align-shiva-nandy-officially-signing-velocity-gaming-sky-route)
- Trinity Gaming signed Blind Esports' roster specifically to build South Indian influencer marketing (Feb 2026) — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2321103) (URL attribution from search snippet; verify)
- Lumikai: Indian gamers want cultural touchpoints including language; Loco hosts Hindi and local-language streamers — [Naavik podcast with Salone Sehgal](https://naavik.co/podcast/salone-sehgal/)
- 66% of Indian creators are outside metros (all categories) — cited in prior report from [ISB-Hashfame](https://www.isb.edu/news/press-releases/india-s-creator-economy-enters-a-new-phase-as-non-metro-creators-become-the-majority-reveals-isb-hashfame-report)

### Inferences
- Hinglish captions and titles are the must-have for launch; Tamil and Telugu are the next two languages to support (they have the largest regional gaming channels and dedicated agencies).
- Telugu and Tamil gaming leans heavily on Free Fire MAX, which supports the prior report's "Free Fire second" recommendation.
- Socialhipper's regional engagement table (e.g. Telugu 7.1%) has no source and should not be used.

### Gaps
- No percentage split of Indian gaming creators or watch time by language.
- No source on regional gaming growth rates after 2020.

## 3. How Indian creators stream: phone-only, PC/OBS, vertical live; connectivity constraints

### Takeaway
No survey measures the phone-vs-PC split. Evidence suggests mobile titles (BGMI, Free Fire) dominate and YouTube Live is the main platform; vertical live in the Shorts feed is globally available since Feb 2024. Upload speeds, not downloads, are the constraint for sending multi-GB VODs: mobile upload averages are around 10-14 Mbps.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube vertical live: tested from August 2023, rolled out to all on 7 Feb 2024; going live vertically makes a stream eligible for the Shorts feed; fan funding supported, ads initially not — [Tubefilter, Feb 2024](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/02/08/youtube-vertical-live-streams-shorts-feed-multi-format/); [Tubefilter, Aug 2023](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/08/01/youtube-mobile-live-streaming-youtube-shorts-collabs-qa-stickers/)
- The Shorts live feed is "predominantly" for off-the-cuff phone streams, though OBS is how many gaming channels use the vertical format — [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/youtube-now-displays-live-streams-in-shorts-feeds); [Routenote](https://routenote.com/blog/do-you-want-livestreams-in-your-youtube-shorts-feed/)
- Simultaneous horizontal and vertical broadcasts with unified chat were added in 2025-2026 — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/science/youtube-lets-you-stream-in-both-vertical-and-horizontal-formats/tldr); [PodcastVideos](https://www.podcastvideos.com/articles/youtubes-2026-updates-revolutionize-shorts-live-streaming-monetization)
- YouTube's free AI live highlights only cover vertical streams from the YouTube mobile app, not screencasts (from prior report's Oct 2026 desk check) — see `reports/BGMI AI clipper market validation.md`
- Mobile upload (SpeedGeo Q1 2026, not Ookla): Airtel 13.6 Mbps, Jio 10.8, Vi 8.5, all-ISP average 11.5 Mbps; broadband upload average 56.3 Mbps — [Pulse/SpeedGeo Q1 2026](https://www.pulse.bot/telecom/news/jio-leads-mobile-speeds-while-act-fibernet-tops-broadband-in-india-speedgeo-report-q1-2026-82f5935b-901a-4b3f-944b-d43f39192e7b/)
- Ookla median mobile download reached ~131.77 Mbps (August, year likely 2025) — [TelecomTalk](https://telecomtalk.info/india-sees-median-mobile-download-speed-jump/1000114/)
- CapCut is banned in India since 2020; mobile editors in use include VN (free, no watermark) and KineMaster (₹749/month listed) — [Loopdesk](https://loopdesk.ai/blog/ai-video-editor-pricing-india); [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/indian-creator-tools-ecosystem-2026)

### Inferences
- Rough math (inference): a 4-hour 1080p VOD at ~6 Mbps bitrate is ~10-11 GB; at 11.5 Mbps mobile upload that is about 2 hours of upload, at 56 Mbps broadband about 25 minutes. Importing from a YouTube VOD URL (server-side fetch) avoids this entirely and should be the default path.
- Three setups to support: (a) phone screen-streamed to YouTube Live (horizontal), (b) phone captured into OBS on a PC with facecam overlay, (c) vertical mobile live for the Shorts feed. Creators in (c) also get YouTube's free highlights, so GCM Clipper's edge is strongest in (a) and (b).
- Phone-first creators need a mobile review/approve UI (PWA fits), not a desktop editor.

### Gaps
- No survey of Indian gaming streamers' equipment (phone-only vs PC/OBS vs capture card), or of Streamlabs mobile / Prism adoption in India.
- No 2026 Ookla median upload figure for India found; SpeedGeo figures are averages.

## 4. Willingness to pay: what Indian creators pay for, price anchors, payment habits, freemium norms

### Takeaway
Indian creators already pay in the ₹199-₹999/month band for tools (Canva Pro ₹500/month or ₹4,000/year, Pro Lite ₹199, KineMaster ₹749, Filmora mobile ₹999/month or ₹4,999/year), with UPI AutoPay widely supported. A "full professional stack" is described at ₹2,000-5,000/month. No published survey gives spend by tier or freemium conversion rates for Indian creator tools.

### Cited Findings
- Canva India (pricing page seen 16 Oct 2026): Pro ₹500/month or ₹4,000/year; Pro Lite ₹199/month or ₹1,590/year; Business ₹680/month or ₹6,800/year per person; UPI AutoPay supported for Pro (not Business) — [AppAdvisor](https://appadvisor.in/software/canva); [SwapBiswas](https://swapbiswas.com/blog/canva-pro-price-in-india/); [Loopdesk](https://loopdesk.ai/blog/ai-video-editor-pricing-india)
- Other INR prices (Oct 2026, vendor-written article by Loopdesk): Adobe Premiere ₹733.96/month (₹389 first-year offer); Premiere Mobile ₹799/month or ₹6,900/year; Filmora Pro mobile ₹999/month or ₹4,999/year; Microsoft 365 Personal (Clipchamp premium) ₹689/month; DaVinci Resolve Studio ₹31,980 one-time; Descript, Kapwing, VEED are USD-only — [Loopdesk](https://loopdesk.ai/blog/ai-video-editor-pricing-india)
- FluxNote (vendor page, Mar 2026): KineMaster ₹749/month, Premiere Rush ₹899/month; VN, DaVinci and YouTube Studio used free; "full professional stack" ₹2,000-5,000/month; advice is to start free and add paid tools as revenue grows — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/indian-creator-tools-ecosystem-2026)
- vidIQ ~$7.50/month and TubeBuddy ~$3-4.50/month list prices (USD); an undated Airtel page cites TubeBuddy from ₹249/year; Streamlabs core free with premium add-ons; no current INR price found for vidIQ or Streamlabs Ultra — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.in/compare/172520/182133/tubebuddy/vs/vidiq); [Airtel blog](https://www.airtel.in/blog/?p=27871)
- RBI e-mandate rules (April 2026, via MediaNama): recurring debits up to ₹15,000 need no extra authentication; a pre-debit notice is required at least 24 hours before — [Loopdesk](https://loopdesk.ai/blog/ai-video-editor-pricing-india); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/subscriptions/)
- Google Play supports UPI AutoPay for subscriptions in India; Paddle added UPI AutoPay in 2026 and advises keeping renewals under ₹15,000 — [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/now-pay-for-subscriptions-via-upi-on-google-play/); [Paddle changelog 2026](https://developer.paddle.com/changelog/2026/upi-autopay/)
- UPI trial mechanics: Recurly does not support forced trial-to-paid conversion with UPI and blocks 100% coupons because a mandate must be created at signup — [Recurly docs](https://docs.recurly.com/docs/upi-autopay)
- Vendor claims (not independent): card-gated trials underperform because credit-card penetration is ~8%; UPI AutoPay gives 2-3x sign-up conversion vs eNACH (Razorpay); local INR pricing gives 2.5x conversion lift (EximPe) — [Vmobify](https://vmobify.com/blog/upi-autopay-app-subscriptions); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/subscriptions/); [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/saas-subscriptions-india)
- Indian SMB SaaS price advice (blogs, no data): free or ₹299-499 entry, ₹999-1,999 mid, ₹2,999-4,999 premium; ₹999/month "sweet spot" claimed — [Rajesh R Nair](https://rajeshrnair.com/blog/software/saas/micro-saas-ideas-india.html); [Playto](https://www.playto.so/blogs/how-to-price-your-saas-for-indian-vs-international-customers-in-2026); [24Squad](https://www.24squad.in/blog/b2b-saas-pricing-india-2026/)
- Consumer-app style anchor recommended by one pricing blog: ₹149/month, ₹349/quarter, ₹999/year, ₹99 first-quarter promo (unsourced) — [ProductGrowth.in](https://productgrowth.in/prompts/consumer-subscription-price/)
- Global freemium benchmarks (consumer productivity 1-2%, AI-native 6-8% median) and BGMI Clipper's ₹499-699 one-time price are in the prior report.

### Inferences
- Price anchors that creators already recognise: ₹199 (Canva Pro Lite), ₹499-500 (Canva Pro), ₹749-999 (mobile video editors), ₹4,000-5,000/year (annual Canva/Filmora). A three-step ladder of roughly ₹0 (free, limited hours) / ₹499 / ₹999 per month, with annual at ~8-10 months' price, matches what creators already pay. A team/editor tier at ₹2,499-4,999 fits the SMB band. All are hypotheses.
- Bill in INR through Razorpay/Cashfree UPI AutoPay or Google Play; USD card billing (OpusClip, Eklipse, vidIQ) is itself a barrier GCM Clipper can exploit.
- Use free tier, not card-gated trials, for acquisition; with UPI, a paid first month at a low promo (e.g. ₹99) is easier than a free trial that auto-converts.
- Self-editing small creators (1k-10k) will mostly stay free; revenue comes from creators already paying an editor (₹10k-60k/month per prior report) and from editors/agencies.

### Gaps
- No survey of Indian creators' monthly software spend by subscriber tier.
- No published freemium-to-paid conversion rate for an Indian creator tool.
- Pepper Content, Bol Studio and Typeset pricing were not researched within the tool budget; no Indian creator-SaaS with public gaming-specific pricing found.
- VN Pro and Streamlabs Ultra INR prices not found.

## 5. Adjacent buyers: editors, agencies, esports orgs, talent/MCN, tournament organisers

### Takeaway
The multi-creator buyers are real and identifiable: talent agencies managing hundreds of gaming creators (Trinity Gaming 250+, Sky Route 200+ creators and teams, 8Bit Creatives, Collective Artists Network for Revenant), content houses (S8UL houses ~25 creators), and tournament organisers (Skyesports/JetSynthesys). Freelance gaming editors find clients via Instagram, direct outreach and Fiverr/Twine. No source counts gaming editors in India.

### Cited Findings
- Trinity Gaming says it manages 250+ creators including Dynamo and Shreeman Legend; signed Blind Esports (Feb 2026) — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/?p=2321103) (URL attribution from search snippet; verify)
- Sky Route (Skyesports talent arm, regional focus) reports 200+ creators and teams signed; Skyesports is part of JetSynthesys and claims top South Asian esports viewership — [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/valorant/news-it-feels-fantastic-our-visions-align-shiva-nandy-officially-signing-velocity-gaming-sky-route)
- Collective Artists Network signed a management contract with Revenant Esports (May 2026) — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/collective-artists-network-signs-management-contract-with-revenant-esports/)
- 8Bit Creatives (gaming talent agency) partnered with Rooter for content distribution — [IndianTelevision](https://indiantelevision.com/news-headline/8bit-creatives-partners-with-rooter-to-elevate-indias-gaming-content-landscape/)
- Ebullient Gaming took the influencer mandate for ~24 Sigma talents via AKEF (dated 2022) — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/ebullient-gaming-exclusively-ties-up-with-akef-bags-influencer-management-mandate-of-sigma-talent/)
- S8UL's Mumbai gaming house hosts ~25 creators; S8UL and GodLike were in the Esports Foundation Club Partner Program (40 clubs) for EWC 2026; Orangutan took India's second PUBG Mobile World Cup slot; AMD partnered S8UL and Revenant for creator and esports content (2026) — [Wikipedia: S8UL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S8UL_Esports); [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/sports/indias-esports-powerhouses-s8ul-godlike-to-represent-nation-on-global-stage-at-ewc-2026/); [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/amd-partners-with-s8ul-and-revenant-esports-to-power-indias-competitive-gaming-and-creator-ecosystem/)
- Freelance editors: guides say Instagram is the main place Indian editors find first clients, plus targeted outreach with re-edited sample Shorts to mid-tier YouTubers; Fiverr, Truelancer, Twine and Contra list Indian gaming editors; Freelancer.com gaming-edit bids seen at ~₹1,000-12,500 per project (single-author practitioner blogs; not market data) — [Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/how-to-find-freelance-video-editing-clients-in-india); [Fueler beginners](https://fueler.io/blog/freelance-video-editing-in-india-how-beginners-get-their-first-5-clients-without-connections); [Contra](https://contra.com/hire/video-editors-in-india); [Freelancer](https://www.fi.freelancer.com/projects/adobe-premiere-pro/youtube-gaming-review-video-production-40315185)

### Inferences
- Agencies managing 200-250 creators each are the highest-leverage first B2B targets: one deal covers many channels, and they need multi-channel workspaces, approval flows and reporting, which GCM Clipper's calendar and approval-based posting already target.
- Freelance gaming editors are both a buyer and a channel: give them a client-approval link and multi-client workspace, and each editor brings 3-10 creators.
- Esports orgs (S8UL, GodLike, Revenant, Orangutan, Team Soul) have sponsor-driven content needs around tournaments (AMD, iQOO-style partners), so per-event pricing fits them.

### Gaps
- No count of freelance or agency video editors serving Indian gaming YouTubers.
- No public data on how much agencies or orgs spend on editing tools or headcount.
- Team Soul, GodLike content-team size, and One Impression/Kofluence gaming rosters not checked.

## 6. Distribution channels that reach Indian gaming creators

### Takeaway
Hard evidence on what converts is thin. The identifiable channels are talent agencies and their rosters, freelance editors (Instagram-led), Instagram itself, and in-person gaming events (Comic Con India ~22,000 at Gurugram debut; IGDC Chennai 28-31 Oct 2026; India Gaming Show Chennai April 2026). No source was found on Discord's role, referral norms or phone-brand partnerships specific to creator tools.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram is described as where India's creator economy lives and where editors find clients — [Fueler](https://fueler.io/blog/freelance-video-editing-in-india-how-beginners-get-their-first-5-clients-without-connections)
- Payal Gaming built her audience posting BGMI highlights on Instagram before YouTube (from prior report) — [AFK Gaming](https://afkgaming.com/mobileesports/guide/who-is-payal-gaming)
- IGDC 2026 (18th edition) ran 28-31 Oct 2026 at Chennai Trade Centre, with public "Playground" days 30-31 Oct; tickets ₹299/day — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/igdc-2026-returns-to-chennai-dates-tickets-and-what-to-expect); [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/sci-tech/2026/10/27/gai-igdc-chennai-trade-centre-event.html)
- India Gaming Show 2026 held at Chennai Trade Centre alongside CII Summit FX (April 2026); a secondary listing claims 20,000 visitors, 250 exhibitors (unverified) — [AnimationXpress](https://animationxpress.com/?p=1636946); [JuFair](https://www.jufair.com/information/95504.html)
- Comic Con India's Gurugram debut drew 22,000+ fans (ended 15 March 2026) with gaming zones — [PC-Tablet](https://pc-tablet.com/?p=189704)
- Hardware brand partnerships with orgs exist (AMD with S8UL and Revenant) — [MediaNews4U](https://www.medianews4u.com/amd-partners-with-s8ul-and-revenant-esports-to-power-indias-competitive-gaming-and-creator-ecosystem/)
- One Impression is free for creators and positions itself as India's largest influencer platform (vendor claim via FluxNote) — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/indian-creator-tools-ecosystem-2026)

### Inferences
- Likely order of channels for a zero-budget startup: (1) direct outreach to 10k-500k gaming creators with a free sample of ranked clips from their own last stream (the "re-edited Shorts" pitch editors already use); (2) partner with freelance gaming editors as an affiliate/reseller channel; (3) agency pilots (Trinity, Sky Route, 8Bit); (4) Instagram and YouTube Shorts showing before/after clips; (5) events as a later, cheaper-than-ads presence (IGDC Playground, India Gaming Show).
- Hardware/phone brands sponsor esports orgs, not creator tools; a phone-brand deal is unlikely to be the first channel.

### Gaps
- No data on Discord servers for Indian gaming creators or their size.
- No data on referral or affiliate commission norms for Indian creator software.
- No evidence on college esports as a channel for creator tools.
- No verified 2026 attendance for IGDC or India Gaming Show.
