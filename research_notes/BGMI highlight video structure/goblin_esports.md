# Goblin (Harsh "Goblin" Paudwal, iQOOSouL): how an esports BGMI pro structures highlight videos

Method: public YouTube metadata read with yt-dlp (`--skip-download`) on 12 Nov 2026: the last 79 videos on the main channel's Videos tab (May 2023 to Aug 2026; one members-only video could not be read), the last 30 streams, all 4 main-channel Shorts, the second channel's full list of 526 videos (flat) plus full metadata for its latest 40, auto Hindi captions for 3 highlight videos (a 4th and 5th got HTTP 429), and 5 thumbnails. "Heatmap" means YouTube's "most replayed" data (100 points per video, values 0 to 1). Views and likes are as of 12 Nov 2026. Main channel: [youtube.com/@goblinbgmi](https://www.youtube.com/@goblinbgmi). Second channel: [youtube.com/@goblinsh0rts](https://www.youtube.com/@goblinsh0rts).

## 1. What he uploads, how often, and what gets views (main channel and the second channel)

### Takeaway
The main channel (556K subs) is mostly long scrims livestreams (median 4.4 h) plus about one gameplay highlight video a month, with a median length of 6:05 and a median of about 146K views. A second, official channel, "GoBLiN shorts" (@goblinsh0rts, 131K subs, 526 videos), uploads about 11 long videos a month plus many Shorts. It carries the high-volume edits: tournament highlight cuts, team interviews, LAN vlogs, reactions and funny moments with teammates. On both channels the longer, story-driven edits (8 to 15 min) get more median views than short frag cuts.

### Inferences
- Pacing pattern: a short opening hook (often a clutch or the result), then fight after fight with comms audible, with looting and rotations cut out (flat heatmap stretches like 104 to 251 s in DwnMgerfI_M are probably shortened rotations kept for context), building to the final circle and the WWCD as the payoff. The end spike is the most reliable "most replayed" moment.
- Kill counts in titles run from 5 to 38 solo finishes (median about 9, from 45 numbers parsed from titles), often with a team total (14 to 33). The video is framed around one match's result, not a kill montage.
- The comms are the "esports flavour": callouts, utility, zone and knock calls make full fights legible, so a Kaatclip esports style should keep the comms audio and not bury it under music. The 2023 LAN highlight (aA98jrFkEPQ) is the exception: it is music-backed, likely because LAN comms were not recorded on his device.
- The opening peak (13 of 70 videos) means the first 10 s matter. A cold open on the best moment is the safe default.

## 5. What an automated tool needs to detect and assemble to make videos like these

### Takeaway
To reproduce a Goblin-style esports highlight, Kaatclip must work at match level: split the stream into matches, read each match's kills, knocks, placement and WWCD, score fights, then cut whole fights (with lead-in comms) in timeline order and end on the final circle. It also needs a title, thumbnail and cold-open generator. Most needed signals are on-screen HUD text plus voice-comms activity.

### Cited Findings (evidence that sets each requirement)
- Match-level framing (solo kills, team kills, WWCD, event) is in nearly every title, so the tool must count kills per match and detect the result (WWCD or placement) — [@goblinbgmi/videos](https://www.youtube.com/@goblinbgmi/videos)
- Viewers rewatch the end of the match most (26 of 70 top peaks in the last 15%) and the first seconds (13 of 70 global maxima in the first 10 s). The tool must keep the final fight and WWCD and open with a hook — section 2 heatmap data
- Single standout fights ("Quick 1v3" at 243 to 261 s in [uXLjxu0sz3I](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uXLjxu0sz3I); the 0.86 spike at 293 s in [DwnMgerfI_M](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DwnMgerfI_M)) are mid-video peaks, so the tool needs clutch detection (1vN: own team down to one alive while several enemies are knocked or killed)
- Comms are the narrative track (callouts, utility, knocks, "khatam", "GG") — [nk3MGVXKCFo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nk3MGVXKCFo), [6jIQaYvIOGc](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6jIQaYvIOGc)
- Highlights run 4.5 to 11 min for 1 or 2 matches. Longer story-led videos get more views (7 min and up: 160K to 186K median, against 128K to 133K under 5 min) — section 1

### Inferences (a detection and assembly checklist for an "Esports" preset)
1. **Match segmentation:** detect the lobby or plane (match start), the "Remaining N" counter and the result screen ("Winner Winner Chicken Dinner" or "#k" placement and the elimination summary). Keep the map name for "Erangel + Miramar" style labels.
2. **Per-match stats from the HUD:** own kill-feed entries (finishes) and knocks, the team kill tally, and the result. Rank matches by kills, WWCD and comeback (e.g. a low alive-count before a win).
3. **Fight detection and scoring:** group kill and knock events into fights (gap of about 20 to 30 s or more splits fights). Score by kills, knocks, multi-kills in a short window, 1vN clutch (teammate-down indicators), final-circle position (time in match or remaining count) and comms intensity (speech rate and loudness spikes such as "khatam", "one-H", "nice"). In Goblin's highlights the end fight carries the most weight.
4. **Cut rules:** start each fight about 5 to 15 s before first contact, at the call or rotation, so the comms context is kept. End after the last finish plus a beat for the reaction or "GG". Drop looting, travel and spectating. Keep a short rotation bridge only before the final circles.
5. **Assembly:** a cold open of 3 to 10 s from the top-scored moment; then fights in timeline order; then the final circle and WWCD as the ending. For a session, take the best 1 to 3 matches, put a "Match 1/2" plus map card between them, and aim for 7 to 10 min. Keep game audio and comms on top, with music only under the gaps.
6. **Format:** landscape 16:9 with a clean game feed. Crop or drop the facecam and stream overlay, since Goblin's highlights show none. Separately cut single 1vN clutches into Shorts of 15 to 60 s.
7. **Packaging:** a title from the template "Solo {k} Finishes WWCD | {team_k} Team Finishes | {event/opponent} 🚀" with hook words ("Comeback", "Decider", "1v3", "Domination"). A thumbnail with the creator's face cutout, a big kill number or hook word and a frame from the end fight. Optional hashtags or tags; chapters are not needed in this style.
8. **Funny moments:** for the esports type, route them to a separate "funny" Short or compilation, not into the match highlight (his second channel's pattern).
