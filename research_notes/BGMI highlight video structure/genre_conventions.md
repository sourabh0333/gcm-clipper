# BGMI / PUBG Mobile long-form highlight videos: genre conventions and what an auto-editor needs

Method: public YouTube metadata only, via yt-dlp `--skip-download` (nothing downloaded), 12 Nov 2026. 14 searches ("BGMI highlights", "BGMI stream highlights", "BGMI funny moments", "BGMI 20 kills full match highlights", "BGMI esports highlights", "BGMI live highlights hindi", "BGMI best moments 2025", "BGMI conqueror highlights", "BGMI clutch 1v4 highlights", "BGMI chicken dinner highlights 2025", "BGMI rush gameplay highlights", "BGMI streamer funny moments compilation", "BGMI tournament highlights 2025", and one more), 40 results each, 404 unique videos; goblinbgmi, Jokerkihavelii and LoLzZzGaming removed. Full metadata (duration, views, likes, upload date, description, chapters, "most replayed" heatmap) pulled for 80 hand-picked Indian BGMI highlight-type videos; 78 are 30 minutes or shorter and form the sample below (49 of them uploaded in 2025-2026). Upload lists (last 40 videos) pulled for 14 channels to measure cadence. Sample bias: search ranking favours big and old hits, so view numbers skew high; small creators are under-represented.

## 1. Length, views, cadence, and how a highlight video relates to the source stream

### Takeaway
The genre's centre is 8-10 minutes (median 9.9 min, half the sample between 8.1 and 13.2 min), and 8:00-8:35 is a hard cluster (17 of 78 videos), which matches YouTube's 8-minute mid-roll threshold. 8-10 min videos had the best median views; past 12 min the median falls. Dedicated "stream highlight" channels post about 1 video a week (0.3-1.0/week), daily-streaming entertainers' clip channels 2-2.5/week. The source is usually one session (one or several matches of one stream), and for esports players one tournament day.

### Inferences
- The default target should be 8:00-10:00, with a floor of 8:00 when the material allows (mid-rolls). The owner's 7-10 min idea is right, but 7:xx gives up mid-roll ads, so prefer 8:05-9:30. Longer (12-20 min) only makes sense for "full intense match" or multi-match clutch formats (Bixi OP, KemO, Max Gaming).
- About one long video a week per streamer is the norm; daily streamers with a clip channel do 2-3 a week. A tool producing one per session (per stream day) and letting the creator pick 1-3 a week fits.
- "Per match" works only for exceptional matches (20+ kills, 50 team kills, chicken dinner with a story). Otherwise videos are cut "per session" and themed (one storyline: trolling a friend, randoms, rank push to Conqueror, a tournament day).

### Gaps
- No YouTube Analytics (retention, CTR) is public; views are lifetime totals and depend on channel size, so the per-length comparison is confounded by channel. No per-video split of how much of the stream was used.
- Upload dates from channel listings are approximate (yt-dlp `approximate_date`).

## 2. Recurring structures (cold open, montage, final fight, ending, funny segments, chapters, edits)

### Takeaway
Almost no BGMI highlight video uses YouTube chapters (1 of 78). Three structural families show up. (a) Esports/frag videos: the best kill as a hook in the first seconds, then chronological fights, building to the end zone, WWCD or final fight, which is rewatched. (b) Comedy/entertainment videos: a storyline or theme title, bits strung together, peaks spread through the middle, and viewers drift off before the end. (c) Clutch compilations: ranked or numbered clutches. Intros, outros and background music show up in a minority of descriptions (7 of 78 credit music); editor credits are rare (2 of 78).

### Inferences
- Treat the cold open as mandatory: the 3-8 s of best action (or funniest line) before any intro. Heatmaps show the opening is rewatched in 75% of videos, so whatever is placed there gets seen.
- By style:
  - Esports or challenge: chronological match story (drop → fights with kill-count progress → end zone → WWCD or results), with the ending saved for the strongest fight. The ending is where replay peaks.
  - Comedy: a theme title plus the 6-15 best bits, the strongest in the first third and middle. The end can be a short "one last bit" or a funny death, then a quick outro. Don't burn 60 s on a final fight nobody rewatches.
  - Clutch compilations: numbered and countdown-friendly ("TOP 30 Clutches").
- Chapters aren't a genre convention, but auto-generating them costs nothing and helps search. Optional.
- Visual effects (zooms, memes, sound effects, captions) can't be confirmed from metadata. Descriptions only show that music, intros and outros, and hired editors exist. See gaps.

### Gaps
- No frame-level view of the edits (zoom-ins, meme inserts, sound effects, transitions, subtitles); that needs watching the videos, which this metadata-only pass did not do. Other researchers covering specific channels may have it.
- Heatmap labels are coarse (100 buckets); exact event types at peaks are inferred from title context, not seen.

## 3. Titles and thumbnails

### Takeaway
Titles are mostly ALL CAPS English (49 of 78 are mostly caps), carry emojis (43 of 78: 🔥😂🤣😱💀🥵), and lead with a hard number (kill count, finishes, team kills, "1v4", "Top 30") or a social or storyline hook ("Joker frustrating Snax…", "PLAYING BGMI WITH RANDOMS *WENT WRONG*"). Hindi in Devanagari is rare in titles (1 of 78), though Hinglish wording appears (e.g. "Gameplay nahi नसीब ही खराब है"). Thumbnails weren't analysed.

### Cited Findings

## 4. What viewers rewatch (heatmaps)

### Takeaway
Two clear rewatch zones: the opening (52 of 69 videos above the mean in the first 5%) and the climax in the last 40% (32 of 69 put their single biggest peak there). In esports and kill videos the final fight or WWCD is the most rewatched moment and the ending holds (median end/mean 1.01). Comedy videos peak in the middle and fall off sharply at the end (0.44).

### Cited Findings
- See section 2 for the numbers. Additional peaks tied to specific moments: Scout "54 Team Kills" peaks at 73-74% and 89-90% [J01O8lGVJXM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J01O8lGVJXM); Jonathan "25 FINISHES" two peaks at 59-60% and 93-94% (two big fights) [2oKjXz6TE2Y](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oKjXz6TE2Y); Bixi OP "vs 12+ CONQUEROR STREAMERS" peaks at 89-90% [iYWC2q6VP88](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iYWC2q6VP88); KRAFTON "BMPS 2026 Grand Finals Highlights" (42 min) opening ratio 0.11 but 91-97% peaks at the trophy finish [nfw6YJY7bv8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nfw6YJY7bv8).
- Coarse 20-bucket heatmap shapes (0-9 scale): Jonathan BMPS WWCD [1H9dU3gNW5o](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1H9dU3gNW5o) "4 2 0 0 4 0 0 0 1 1 0 2 1 5 1 1 0 5 2 2" — several separate peaks (one per fight) on a low baseline; Scout XTRA "50+ TEAM KILLS" [UURmovLNuws](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UURmovLNuws) "1 0 0 0 0 0 0 0 0 5 0 …" — one spike mid-video, everything else flat.

### Inferences
- Rewatch peaks are narrow (one fight or one line) on a flat baseline, so videos are watched linearly with spikes at single moments. A good auto-edit spreads 4-6 "spike-worthy" moments about every 60-90 s rather than one big moment and filler.
- For kill-type videos, put the strongest fight (most kills in a short window, a 1vN clutch, or the WWCD-winning fight) last and tease it in the cold open. For comedy, put the strongest bit in the first 30-50% and keep the ending short.

### Gaps
- The heatmap is relative (normalised per video) and missing for low-view videos (9 of 78 had none). It doesn't measure drop-off, so absolute retention is unknown.

## 5. What BGMI's screen offers for match splitting and judging, and existing auto-highlight tools

### Takeaway
I found no public tool or write-up that segments BGMI/PUBG Mobile streams into matches. Public PUBG auto-clippers are PC-only (DOR, SteelSeries Moments, Medal on Windows), and commercial AI clippers don't document PUBG Mobile support. Academic work (X-CLIP per-second classifier) reaches about 91% on PUBG PC events but doesn't segment matches. The match-structure signals therefore have to come from Kaatclip's own HUD reading. Public sources confirm the post-match results screen shows kills, damage, survival time, healing and revives, plus rank.

### Cited Findings
- PUBG Mobile's end-of-match screen gives "a more comprehensive stat breakdown than the PC version" (kills, damage dealt, survival time, health restored, revives), and the career page lists mode, rank, kills and score per match — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/how-view-your-match-results-and-statistics-pubg-mobile).
- Auto-highlight tools for PUBG (PC): DOR records the whole PUBG match and auto-clips every knock and kill (method not described) — [clip.dor.gg](https://clip.dor.gg/en/games/pubg); SteelSeries Moments added PUBG auto-clipping with "Killed", "#1: Winner" and multi-kill triggers — [SteelSeries](https://steelseries.com/blog/how-to-capture-pubg-clips-1170); Medal's automatic event detection is Windows-only, not iOS or Android — [Medal support](https://support.medal.tv/support/solutions/articles/48001167701-what-is-automatic-event-detection-auto-clipping-).

### Inferences (requirements for the assembler; HUD details are domain knowledge, not verified by a public source in this pass)
- Match segmentation states: lobby or spawn island (waiting area, no alive counter or squad HUD) → plane (map overlay with flight path, "Jump" button) → parachute → in-match (alive counter, own kill counter, minimap, zone timer) → death or spectate (player view swaps to a teammate, spectating label) or squad elimination → results screen (placement "#N/100", "WINNER WINNER CHICKEN DINNER" for #1, finishes, damage, survival time, assists, rescues) → back to lobby. A results screen or a lobby re-appearing closes the match. The rising "alive" counter reset to about 100 at the next plane marks a new match.
- Per-match scoring from what the engine reads: own kills and knocks over time (kill feed), placement (players-left at death, or the results screen), WWCD flag, peak kill density (kills in a 60-90 s window, i.e. squad wipes), clutch (teammates down or spectating while the player still fights; 1vN when the kill feed shows a teammate dead or knocked plus multiple enemy kills after), final-zone involvement (players left ≤10-15 when fights happen), and talk or laugh density from the transcript. A "20-kill match" or WWCD match becomes a "match video" candidate. Otherwise group the session's best moments into a "session video".
- Mode detection matters: TDM, Arena and other modes need different templates (no drop, no alive counter in the same form, short rounds). - Dead time to cut: lobby, plane, looting with no talk, long rotations, and spectating after death (unless the streamer is reacting or commentating, which the transcript shows).

## 6. Copyright and originality (commentary, music, reused-content risk)

### Takeaway
Streamers' own highlight channels mostly add no new voice-over. The "commentary" is the streamer's live mic and reactions, and the added value is the edit (selection, storyline, intro and outro, some music). YouTube's reused-content policy targets "clips … edited together with little or no narrative" and promotion of other people's content even with permission. "Content that primarily features the uploading creator" and "substantive editing" are explicitly allowed. A streamer re-editing their own stream is on safe ground, while third-party fan channels re-cutting someone else's stream (DEVILE GAMING, AniFunnies) are the risky kind.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube reused content, allowed: "Content that primarily features the uploading creator in the video", "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary", "Replays of a sports tournament where you explain the moves", and edited footage with audio and visual effects that "demonstrates substantive editing". Not allowed: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative", "Content uploaded many times by other creators", "Promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)". The policy applies channel-wide and even with permission — [YouTube Help: YPP policies (reused content)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
- 15 July 2025 update: "repetitious content" was renamed "inauthentic content" (mass-produced or repetitive), and YouTube said the reused-content policy wasn't changed — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/youtube-issues-clarification-on-monetisation-policy-update-after-creator-backlash-145327.html); [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/).
- Third-party guides: cutting highlights from your own long-form content fits what reviewers expect, but raw, unedited stream uploads raise the risk. Reviews look at the whole channel — [air.io](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected); [mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-reused-content). (Secondary sources; treat as practitioner opinion.)
- Practice in the sample: own-channel highlight videos carry copyright notices ("Copyright © 2026 sc0ut - All Rights Reserved", Scout XTRA [UURmovLNuws](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UURmovLNuws); Jonathan [2oKjXz6TE2Y](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2oKjXz6TE2Y)). Shreeman Legend's description warns re-uploaders: "please do not use any recent content (from the last 30 days) without prior permission. Unauthorized use may result in a copyright strike" [2rJ94d1sxPU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2rJ94d1sxPU). KemO gives free use of gameplay clips but not thumbnails or intros [6fTlj64Jx1g](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6fTlj64Jx1g).

### Inferences
- For the creator's own streams, the auto-edit should still show "substantive editing" to be safe: a cold open, a storyline title, captions or text callouts (kill counter, "1v3 CLUTCH", "#1"), music beds in quiet stretches, a branded intro and outro, and selection with narrative. Never a raw stream chunk. The streamer's live voice is the commentary; keep transcript-rich moments.
- Music: use only royalty-free or YouTube Audio Library tracks (ship a bundled library), and duck under the streamer's voice. Popular film songs and meme audio risk Content ID claims (revenue goes to the claimant rather than a strike, per [mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-reused-content)).
- In-game copyrighted audio (BGMI voice packs, lobby music) is accepted across the genre (whole videos are built around voice packs: "SNAX REACT ON NOT YOUR TYPE VOICE PACK" [ANJ3V0QGCmw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ANJ3V0QGCmw)). No public KRAFTON content policy was checked in this pass.
- Kaatclip should only process the user's own streams (or those they have rights to). Third-party re-cut channels are the "reused content" risk case.
