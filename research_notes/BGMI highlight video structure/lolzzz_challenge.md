### Inferences
- The live stream is the product, and the edited highlight is a periodic "trophy" post. A highlight is cut only when a session produces a headline number: a new kill record (50+ solo kills), a completed or failed challenge, or a rank milestone. Ordinary sessions get no highlight, only the stream VOD. For Kaatclip this means the "highlight video" output should be triggered by a standout match, not made for every stream.
- The 72-kill video went up on 25 Oct 2026, after streams titled "60 SOLO KILLS KAB HONGE?" (21 Oct), "70 SOLO KILLS TODAY?" (23 Oct) and "AAJ 70 SOLO KILLS HOKE RAHENGE" (24 Oct). The stream's stated goal ran over several days, and the highlight is the payoff of that run.
- About 10-13 minutes is the format norm (interquartile range 9-14 min). This is a little longer than the owner's "7-10 minutes" target. A 10-12 min default with a 7-15 min range matches him.

## Anatomy of the top highlight / challenge videos

### Takeaway
A kill-record video is **one match**, not several. It runs 10-13 minutes, with no chapters and usually no description. It opens with a 20-40 second cold open (goal stated, final number flashed), then plays the match in chronological order and **ends at the final kill and the celebration**. A challenge video with a multi-match goal ("100 kills in 3 matches", "Platinum to #1 Conqueror") runs 24-31 minutes and keeps the goal and the running count spoken aloud.

### Inferences
- **Story template, single-match record:** (1) a cold open of 20-45 s: the multi-day goal said by him ("target 70"), then the final number flashed ("72") over music; (2) the match runs chronologically from drop to the record kill, with his live voice and squad comms left in as the commentary track; (3) the running count is mentioned at milestones ("7 more"); (4) the final kill, then a 20-30 s celebration, then the video ends. Since the video fits a whole ~25-30 min match into ~11-13 min, about half the match is cut. What is left looks like every fight (kills and knocks); looting, rotations and driving are trimmed to short bridges. The editing compresses the match rather than picking only the best moments: all 72 kills must be reachable for the count to be credible.
- **Story template, multi-match challenge:** goal stated by him at 0:00 (often sourced from a Super Chat / donation) → match 1 → a spoken checkpoint ("32 done, 68 left") → match 2 → "final match" framing → verdict. Each match gets about 7-9 min, so a 3-match challenge runs about 24 min. The suspense rests on the remaining-count arithmetic.
- **Rank-push journey:** the stakes (points needed, matches left, the struggle) → a montage of the decisive matches → reaching the rank or position → celebration. It uses an in-game stats or rank card as proof, mostly in the thumbnail.
- Kills are not counted on an editor overlay in what I can observe; the count comes from his voice and the in-game kill counter. Whether the edit adds an on-screen kill counter cannot be confirmed without watching.

## What an automated tool would need to detect and assemble to make these from a raw stream

### Takeaway
The unit is the **match** and the **session goal**, not the 30-second moment. Kaatclip would need to split a 5-6 h stream into matches and read the kill count (and ideally knocks) per match from the HUD. It would then pick the record match, or the set of matches that belong to the stated goal, and compress that match to about 10-13 min by keeping every fight and cutting the downtime. The result needs a cold open with the goal and the final number, and should end on the final kill plus the reaction.

### Cited Findings
